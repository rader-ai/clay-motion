# How Aardman handles clay (plasticine) stop motion

Prepared 2026-10-17 for the AI clay studio (Blender, puppets rendered from code).

**Scope covered:** Wallace & Gromit shorts and features, Chicken Run (2000) and Dawn of the Nugget (2023), Early Man (2018), Shaun the Sheep (series and films), Creature Comforts, Morph, and Aardman clay projects announced or released 2025 to 2026.

**How to read this:**
- Every factual line carries a source link. Lines marked **[U]** are unverified, inferred, or my own arithmetic or judgment.
- Where sources disagree, both figures are given and flagged **CONFLICT**.
- Quotes are short phrases only; everything else is paraphrased.
- Numbers are as reported by the cited outlet. Press numbers for output per animator vary a lot by film and outlet (see section 4.2).

**Known gaps (be aware before relying on this):**
- The web search budget ran out partway through the research, so a few leads were not followed up. A follow-up message can resume them.
- The book "Cracking Animation" (Lord and Sibley) could not be reached in full text; nothing here is quoted from it directly.
- The befores & afters piece with Will Becher on Vengeance Most Fowl (clay, 3D printing, motion control) is paywalled; only its teaser and a companion article were readable.
- No source found for: an exact mouth count for Wallace; Aardman using alcohol to clean clay; digital-era f-stop values; the step-by-step colour-mixing procedure.

### 1.2 What the material is

- HUE describes the revived Newplast as oil-based, non-drying, non-setting, with no water in it. It is firm enough to hold shape indefinitely and resists cracking. [HUE FAQ](https://huehd.com/support/faqs/faq-category/clay/), [HUE Newplast FAQ](https://huehd.com/ufaqs/newplast/)
- **CONFLICT on the base:** Oxford Times calls it a "nylon-reinforced, air-drying" clay; a WSJ-video summary calls it "wax-based"; HUE (the actual manufacturer) says oil-based and non-drying; Animation Supplies says Chroma Clay is wax-based "unlike Newplast"; a model maker at Aardman describes American Plasticine as the wax-based one. HUE and the Chroma Clay makers are the most reliable on this; treat "air-drying" and "wax-based Newplast" as wrong. [Oxford Times](https://www.oxfordtimes.co.uk/news/23934047.wallace-gromit-studio-modelling-clay-supplier-closes/), [TKSST](https://thekidshouldseethis.com/post/the-clay-that-makes-aardman-animations-come-to-life), [HUE FAQ](https://huehd.com/support/faqs/faq-category/clay/), [Cartoon Brew Chroma Clay](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/tools/new-stop-motion-plasticine-chroma-clay-260899.html), [AUB (Parkyn)](https://graduateschool.aub.ac.uk/top-news/i-ve-worked-in-claymation-for-20-years-here-s-what-i-use-to-make-morph-gromit-and-shaun-the-s.html)
- A secondary explainer says the filler is calcium carbonate bound by an oily material, and that the clay stays pliable under the hot filming lights. [Book and Film Globe](https://bookandfilmglobe.com/?p=23560)
- The Science and Media Museum says the clay keeps its surface textures under hot studio lights, which is why Aardman chose it. [Science and Media Museum](https://blog.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/surprising-stories-about-aardman-animations/)
- Aardman sells the material in 500 g bars (as reported). [Book and Film Globe](https://bookandfilmglobe.com/?p=23560) **[U]** (secondary; may refer to a consumer product)
- Aardman also uses American Plasticine (brighter, wax-based, meltable into moulds) on some projects. [AUB (Parkyn)](https://graduateschool.aub.ac.uk/top-news/i-ve-worked-in-claymation-for-20-years-here-s-what-i-use-to-make-morph-gromit-and-shaun-the-s.html)

### 1.3 Colour mixing and consistency

- Aardman uses "100s of pounds" of clay on a feature, and the bulk clay from the factory goes through several processes before animators get it. The stated goal is that Gromit's fur is the same shade in frame 6800 as in frame 1. [Kottke, summarising an Adam Savage visit](https://kottke.org/18/02/behind-the-scenes-look-at-mixing-the-clay-for-wallace-and-gromit) (the mixing detail itself is in a video I could not read)
- A 2019 report says the Newplast is "mixed on site on an industrial scale", with chalk added so it holds up under the hot studio lights. [Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/arts-and-culture/film-and-tv/aardman-uncovered-behind-the-scenes-on-a-shaun-the-sheep-movie-farmageddon-1405412)
- HUE warns that small batch-to-batch differences in hardness and colour occur. Mixed colours blend fastest by tearing and folding, and are conditioned by kneading when cold. [HUE FAQ](https://huehd.com/support/faqs/faq-category/clay/), [HUE guide](https://huehd.com/become-a-claymation-pro/)
- Colour is also managed on the production side: Dawn of the Nugget had a colour script with a reference image for each key establishing shot. [British Cinematographer (Copping)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/charles-copping-emchicken-run-dawn-of-the-nugget-em/)
- Colour can shift on camera: Lu-La's blue read purple under greenscreen, so the team tested many shades. [Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/2020/01/a-wild-and-woolly-adventure-aardman-brings-intergalactic-fun-to-farmageddon)
- Park personally chose the grass colour of the Early Man pitch. [Shoot](https://shootonline.com/?p=46923)
- Exact mixing recipe, ratios, and batch controls: not found. **[U]**

### 1.4 Behaviour under hot lights

- Older lighting was hot. Crossingham: modern LEDs mean you "don't cook the animators with 10K lights like we had to previously". [Cartoon Brew Connect](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/cartoon-brew-connect/wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-filmmakers-in-conversation-with-cartoon-brew-exclusive-video-245814.html)
- Riddett: LEDs suit shots that burn for a week; older incandescent lamps could blow mid-shot. [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)
- 1990s practice: low-wattage lamps (a 1998 article says 12-watt) because standard lights soften the clay, with 1/2 second or longer exposures. [CS Monitor 1998](https://www.csmonitor.com/1998/0512/051298.home.home.1.html)
- Chicken Run (2000): stop-motion exposures of 1/4 to 1/2 second were typical, so little light was needed; the key light in one set was a 2K fresnel. [Live Design](https://www.livedesignonline.com/run-chicken-run)
- Were-Rabbit: some shots used four-second exposures, lit only by candle-level sources. [AC Oct 2005, p3](https://theasc.com/magazine/oct05/curse/page3.html)
- Oliver (DP): "only so many ways to light a piece of Plasticine" before it loses texture, and the gap between exposed and overexposed is very small. [AC Oct 2005, p2](https://theasc.com/magazine/oct05/curse/page2.html)
- Shaun the Sheep Movie: fleece was stiffened with diluted PVA glue so it would not "boil" under the studio lights or shift when touched. [Press and Journal](https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/entertainment/480645/lifestyle-keeping-shaun-sheep-shape/)
- Consumer guidance from HUE (not Aardman): do not expose above 50 C; strong light can dull colours. **[U]** as it applies to studio practice. [HUE FAQ](https://huehd.com/support/faqs/faq-category/clay/)

### 1.5 Dust, lint, dirt, cleaning

- Early Man puppet-making team leader Nigel Leach: "The plasticine has a tendency to pick up dirt and dust." [Animation Magazine (Early Man)](https://animationmagazine.net/?p=235675)
- Handling leaves grease and dirt on the clay, so animators sometimes swap in a clean replacement head; the dirty head is scraped and cleaned. Worn-out clay is thrown away, armatures are reused. [CS Monitor 1998](https://www.csmonitor.com/1998/0512/051298.home.home.1.html)
- Baby wipes are the cleaning tool people mention: an animator's first Aardman memory is the smell of clay and the wipes used on it; Shaun Movie producers kept wipes on hand. [AWN](https://awn.com/animationworld/aardman-50-inside-clay-felt-and-fingerprints), [Press and Journal](https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/entertainment/480645/lifestyle-keeping-shaun-sheep-shape/)
- Storage advice from an Aardman model maker: keep dust and sunlight off and it keeps for decades. [AUB (Parkyn)](https://graduateschool.aub.ac.uk/top-news/i-ve-worked-in-claymation-for-20-years-here-s-what-i-use-to-make-morph-gromit-and-shaun-the-s.html)
- Alcohol: **no source found** saying Aardman cleans with alcohol. HUE's consumer FAQ says paraffin or nail-varnish remover soften the clay and are flammable. **[U]**
- Lint and hair: no Aardman statement found. Early Man hair and fur twitch from handling, which Park welcomed. [Cartoon Brew interview](https://cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/interview-nick-park-earthy-humor-art-early-man-156717.html)

### 1.6 Cracking

- Newplast is prized for resisting cracking. [HUE announcement](https://huehd.com/hue-rolls-out-new-product-line/), [HUE Newplast FAQ](https://huehd.com/ufaqs/newplast/)
- Cold makes it hard and crumbly; aged clay can harden or turn brittle. (Consumer guidance.) [HUE FAQ](https://huehd.com/support/faqs/faq-category/clay/)
- It is foam latex, not clay, that "dries out, cracks" over time; that is a main reason silicone replaced it. [Befores and Afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/01/17/a-mix-of-old-and-new/)

### 1.7 Shine and matte control

- Dawn of the Nugget: chicken puppets were dusted with finely sifted icing sugar (powdered sugar) to get an even matte finish and cut shine on camera. [TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/production-post/counting-your-chickens-fun-facts-from-the-production-of-chicken-run-dawn-of-the-nugget), [What to Watch](https://www.whattowatch.com/features/chicken-run-2-dawn-of-the-nugget-15-egg-citing-facts)
- It replaced silica powder, which was used before but is toxic and needs protective gear. Kate Anderson (head of puppets): it gives "a perfect matte surface if you get it right". [Variety via AOL](https://www.aol.com/inside-making-chicken-run-dawn-180000163.html), [Variety via Yahoo](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/inside-making-chicken-run-dawn-180000647.html)
- Many silicone bodies use a special non-shiny silicone formula. [Cartoon Brew Connect](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/cartoon-brew-connect/wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-filmmakers-in-conversation-with-cartoon-brew-exclusive-video-245814.html)
- Riddett tested a silicone Gromit body with plasticine hands under white, blue and warm light because the two materials read differently under some lights. [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)
- Chalk in the clay mix (1.3) is a second reported lever. [Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/arts-and-culture/film-and-tv/aardman-uncovered-behind-the-scenes-on-a-shaun-the-sheep-movie-farmageddon-1405412)

### 2.2 Armatures and rigs

- Gromit is built from a stainless steel skeleton up. [Kottke](https://kottke.org/16/05/how-gromit-from-wallace-and-gromit-is-built)
- Ball-and-socket joints sized to character weight; Farmer John reuses Trumper's armature from the first Shaun film. Each character gets an "MOT" where key animators try to break it before it is mass-produced. [Fandom feature](https://www.fandom.com/articles/aardman-shaun-the-sheep-behind-the-scenes)
- Puppets are bolted or nailed to the set floor for each shot. [Shoot](https://shootonline.com/?p=46923), [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/showbiz/2026/09/04/shaun-the-sheeps-woolraising-halloween-romp-marks-50-years-of-claycrafted-stopmotion-magic/233879)
- Rigs and wires no longer have to be hidden from the lens; they are painted out in post, which frees animators. Early Man's Dug and Hognob rig was removed in post. [MPA interview](https://www.motionpictures.org/2025/01/wallace-and-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-directors-nick-park-merlin-crossingham-go-back-to-the-bakehouse/), [VFX Voice (Early Man)](https://vfxvoice.com/?p=4501)

### 2.3 Rebuilding and re-sculpting between shots

- Puppets are duplicated heavily: Dawn had up to 18 Ginger puppets on standby (Variety) or 16 each of Ginger and Molly (Animation Magazine) **CONFLICT**; Early Man ended with 18 identical Dug puppets; Farmageddon had 22 Shauns; VMF had 11 Feathers and 10 Norbots. [Variety via Yahoo](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/inside-making-chicken-run-dawn-180000647.html), [Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/?p=3243157), [VFX Voice (Early Man)](https://vfxvoice.com/?p=4501), [Fandom feature](https://www.fandom.com/articles/aardman-shaun-the-sheep-behind-the-scenes), [TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/production-post/cracking-creation-fun-facts-from-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl)
- Leach: it is "a constant process of renewing the mouth sets and repairing the heads." [Animation Magazine (Early Man)](https://animationmagazine.net/?p=235675)
- Each puppet is micro-adjusted multiple times within a shot. [Animation Magazine (Early Man)](https://animationmagazine.net/?p=235675)
- If a character changes clothes, you generally have to re-sculpt. [Press and Journal](https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/entertainment/480645/lifestyle-keeping-shaun-sheep-shape/)
- Tears are made from jelly, glycerin or Vaseline depending on shot length and applied with a pin, drop by drop. [Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/2020/01/a-wild-and-woolly-adventure-aardman-brings-intergalactic-fun-to-farmageddon)
- Fine sculpting is also done live in front of the camera, with pre-built mouth shapes blended into faces. [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)

### 2.4 Thumbprints and tool marks: left in or smoothed?

Deliberately left in, and sometimes added:

- Shaun the Sheep's official site says model makers' fingerprints are often visible and are an Aardman trademark. [shaunthesheep.com](https://shaunthesheep.com/about/)
- Park on Early Man: "I was insistent that we retain the fingerprints", while scaling production up. [Dragonframe blog](https://www.dragonframe.com/blog/early-man-aardman-animations/), [Cineworld interview](https://stgttrp-www.cineworld.ie/static/en/ie/blog/early-man-aardman-nick-park-interview)
- On Early Man, animators' fingerprints show on faces, clothes and bodies, magnified on screen. [Cartoon Brew interview](https://cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/interview-nick-park-earthy-humor-art-early-man-156717.html) Telegraph: fingerprints on the faces add subtle life. [Telegraph via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-daily-telegraph/20180115/282196536359013)
- Were-Rabbit: the maker's thumbprints are visible on Gromit's forehead when his brow creases. Park: "fingerprints come with clay"; he did not want it "too slick". [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/4950560), [CS Monitor 2005](https://csmonitor.com/2005/1007/p13s01-almo.html)
- Dawn of the Nugget: Sam Fell says animators are encouraged to "leave their thumbprints on the faces". [MovieMaker](https://www.moviemaker.com/chicken-run-dawn-of-the-nugget-peter-lord/)
- Sarah Cox (Aardman chief creative director): expressive facial moments are sculpted individually in clay, which is where the thumbprints show. [ShortList](https://www.shortlist.com/entertainment/aardmans-sir-peter-lord-on-his-journey-from-a-kitchen-table-to-hollywood-rocket-packs-and-how-the-studio-favours-thumbiness-over-ai)
- Vengeance Most Fowl: Crossingham says fingerprints can still be put into Wallace's sleeve, "that would have been modeling clay, but now we can use a silicone version." So prints are authored into silicone costume parts on purpose. [MPA interview](https://www.motionpictures.org/2025/01/wallace-and-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-directors-nick-park-merlin-crossingham-go-back-to-the-bakehouse/)
- Farmageddon: thumbprints from the original clay sculpt are kept in the silicone puppets. [Fandom feature](https://www.fandom.com/articles/aardman-shaun-the-sheep-behind-the-scenes), Scotsman: the final film keeps imperfections like fingerprints. [Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/arts-and-culture/film-and-tv/aardman-uncovered-behind-the-scenes-on-a-shaun-the-sheep-movie-farmageddon-1405412)
- Beast of Mossy Bottom era: Sean Clarke defines "thumbiness" as the visible hand of the maker, "sometimes literally in the fingerprints in the clay". [ShortList](https://www.shortlist.com/entertainment/aardmans-sir-peter-lord-on-his-journey-from-a-kitchen-table-to-hollywood-rocket-packs-and-how-the-studio-favours-thumbiness-over-ai)

Smoothed, then reversed (important history):

- On Chicken Run (2000) the team spent considerable time removing thumbprints; audiences later connected with the handmade quality, and on Were-Rabbit Park told the team not to fuss. [Alternative Magazine, Crossingham 2009](https://alternativemagazineonline.co.uk/2009/11/27/interview-in-converstion-with-merlin-crossingham-lead-animator-at-aardman-animation/)
- Ian Whitlock (animation director) is reported as saying the studio once smoothed prints away and it drained life from the characters, so they are now deliberately kept. [TKSST summary of a WSJ video](https://thekidshouldseethis.com/post/the-clay-that-makes-aardman-animations-come-to-life) (secondary)
- AWN: Becher says Aardman does not want models to be perfect and that "little finger marks and prints" can help define a character. [AWN](https://awn.com/animationworld/aardman-50-inside-clay-felt-and-fingerprints)

What I could not find: any source giving a ridge spacing, print size, depth, or rule about where prints must or must not appear. The user's "0.41 to 0.46 mm" is consistent with real animator fingers pressed into a 15 to 18 cm puppet, but that is my inference **[U]**.

### 2.5 "Boil"

- Aardman rarely uses the word. The one explicit use: Shaun Movie fleece was stiffened with diluted PVA so it would not "boil" under lights or shift when touched. [Press and Journal](https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/entertainment/480645/lifestyle-keeping-shaun-sheep-shape/)
- Contrast: Park welcomed twitchy hair and fur on Early Man (Hognob's fur, Dug's hair), and admires the twitching fur of the original King Kong, which came from handling on every frame. [Cartoon Brew interview](https://cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/interview-nick-park-earthy-humor-art-early-man-156717.html), [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/hereandnow/2018/02/20/nick-park-early-man)
- Telegraph: hair is gently shifted from frame to frame as if brushed by unseen hands. [Telegraph via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-daily-telegraph/20180115/282196536359013)
- Mechanism: surface change can only happen when an animator touches the puppet, which on twos means once per pose. That is physics, not a quoted rule **[U]**.
- Net: Aardman controls boil on some materials (stiffened fleece, cast silicone) and embraces it on others (clay faces, hair). It is a per-material choice, not "minimal everywhere".

### 3.2 How lip sync is done

- Voice is recorded first and acts as a springboard for the physical performance. Wallace's actor Ben Whitehead recorded about 22.5 hours of takes; 432 lines; one line was recorded 44 ways; the directors spent 216 hours choosing. [Cultbox](https://cultbox.co.uk/features/wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-the-creatives-speak), [TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/production-post/cracking-creation-fun-facts-from-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl), [Alternative Magazine](https://alternativemagazineonline.co.uk/2009/11/27/interview-in-converstion-with-merlin-crossingham-lead-animator-at-aardman-animation/)
- Chicken Run (2000): animators worked from dope sheets with frame-by-frame phonetic breakdowns plus a video-assist system for precise lip sync. [AC Aug 2000, p2](https://theasc.com/magazine/aug00/coop/pg2.htm)
- Dragonframe handles capture and playback; animators review footage in real time. [Post Magazine](https://www.postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2024/November-December-2024/Animation-I-Wallace-Gromit-Vengeance-Most-Fowl-I.aspx)
- Park acts every scene on video for the animators (Early Man); Farmageddon animators acted scenes 10 to 20 times and made 3 to 4 filmed versions per scene; Crossingham: "Live-action reference mitigates the errors", with a reshoot rate of "one-percent". [Shoot](https://shootonline.com/?p=46923), [VFX Voice (Farmageddon)](https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13121757), [Fandom feature](https://www.fandom.com/articles/aardman-shaun-the-sheep-behind-the-scenes), [Cartoon Brew Connect](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/cartoon-brew-connect/wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-filmmakers-in-conversation-with-cartoon-brew-exclusive-video-245814.html)
- Creature Comforts (1989/90): real unscripted interviews are matched to clay animals; the crew kept pauses, yawns and mumbles. [Cartoon Research](https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/talk-to-the-animals-the-35th-anniversary-of-creature-comforts/), [Science and Media Museum](https://blog.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/surprising-stories-about-aardman-animations/)
- X-sheet specifics, per-phoneme frame holds, and whether mouths change on single frames: **not found**. **[U]**

### 3.3 Eyes and blinks

- Early Man eyes are solid resin with a tiny pinhole so animators can move them with a pin. [VFX Voice (Early Man)](https://vfxvoice.com/?p=4501) Early Man noses and eyes are hard plastic that animators grip. [Shoot](https://shootonline.com/?p=46923)
- Dawn: Suzy Parr swaps painted, fingernail-sized eyes into Ginger's clay sockets every two frames (brainwashing sequence); they considered CG and chose this because "the effects look much more real". [Variety via AOL](https://www.aol.com/inside-making-chicken-run-dawn-180000163.html)
- VMF: 600 eyes were made; Feathers McGraw's black glass pin-head eyes needed pins exactly 2.77 mm across, found by sorting 50 pots of pins. The Kitten reuses Ginger's eyes from Dawn. [TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/production-post/cracking-creation-fun-facts-from-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl)
- Key lights are used to put glints in Feathers' glass eye-beads, and the team scrutinised his blink counts. [Cartoon Brew Connect](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/cartoon-brew-connect/wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-filmmakers-in-conversation-with-cartoon-brew-exclusive-video-245814.html), [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)
- Norbot's eyes are locked and still so he reads as a machine; his early moving eyebrows looked too human and were removed. [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/celebrating-the-inventiveness-of-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/), [MPA interview](https://www.motionpictures.org/2025/01/wallace-and-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-directors-nick-park-merlin-crossingham-go-back-to-the-bakehouse/)
- Farmageddon: sorrow is played with small moves (an eye dart, drooping ears) rather than big expressions; a dropped mouth or eyelid could get lost in cramped sets. [Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/2020/01/a-wild-and-woolly-adventure-aardman-brings-intergalactic-fun-to-farmageddon)

### 3.4 Brows and how Gromit acts without a mouth

- Gromit has no mouth. Park had planned one, found on the first animation that eyebrows and body language were more powerful, and decided on the spot he would never speak. [Kottke (summarising Crossingham's guide)](https://kottke.org/16/05/how-gromit-from-wallace-and-gromit-is-built)
- Emotion comes from brows and body language, "rather than animating eyelids". [Cartoon Brew Connect](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/cartoon-brew-connect/wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-filmmakers-in-conversation-with-cartoon-brew-exclusive-video-245814.html)
- Gromit relies on sideways glances and eye-rolls. [Film Hub Wales PDF](https://filmhubwales.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/ANIM18-WALLACE-_-GROMIT.pdf)
- A 2005 NPR piece notes the filmmaker's thumbprints on Gromit's forehead when the brow creases. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/4950560)
- VMF: animators got a three-week refresher focused on Gromit. [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/celebrating-the-inventiveness-of-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)

## 4. Animation timing

### 4.1 Ones vs twos

- Vengeance Most Fowl (Post Magazine): 24 fps capture; characters animated on twos, about 12 movements per second; camera moves shot on ones; animators use a "one-and-a-half" method to keep character motion in sync with the ones-based camera moves, which is technically hard. [Post Magazine](https://www.postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2024/November-December-2024/Animation-I-Wallace-Gromit-Vengeance-Most-Fowl-I.aspx)
- Dawn of the Nugget: Sam Fell calls two frames per move "twos" and says it adds "texture"; for beginners he recommends "twos, if not fours"; the film's puppet animation runs at 12 poses per second. [MovieMaker](https://www.moviemaker.com/chicken-run-dawn-of-the-nugget-peter-lord/)
- Dawn: the team "start off in doubles"; switching to singles mid-shot would be noticeable; CG elements are matched to doubles too. [Skwigly](https://www.skwigly.co.uk/chicken-run-dawn-of-the-nugget-interview-with-suzy-fagan-parr/)
- Farmageddon: target 24 fps and 1.6 to 2 seconds per day; complex camera moves required more "singles". [Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/2020/01/a-wild-and-woolly-adventure-aardman-brings-intergalactic-fun-to-farmageddon)
- Shaun's official site says it takes 12 to 24 frames to make one second of animation (i.e. twos or ones). [shaunthesheep.com](https://shaunthesheep.com/about/)
- Wrong Trousers train chase: camera hand-nudged by roughly 3 inches per frame; the train moved about 10 cm per frame (roughly 300 mph at scale); the test at 60 mph looked ponderous. [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett/), [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/the-making-of-the-wrong-trousers-dream-teams)
- Park on the Wrong Trousers chase: wanted "the fastest thing I've ever seen in stop motion"; the handmade, erratic nature is what gave it charm and modern methods would smooth it. [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/celebrating-the-inventiveness-of-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)
- Direct quotes from Nick Park, Peter Lord, Merlin Crossingham or Sam Fell stating a ones/twos policy for lip sync or fast action: **not found** beyond the above. **[U]**

### 5.2 Apertures and exposure

- Were-Rabbit crews sometimes worked at T22 for maximum depth of field, with puppets "positioned very close to the lens". [AC Oct 2005, p3](https://theasc.com/magazine/oct05/curse/page3.html) T22 is about f/20 (my arithmetic, assuming about 85% lens transmission) **[U]**.
- Chicken Run (2000): the crew "stop the lens down as much as possible" because depth of field at close range is shallow. [Live Design](https://www.livedesignonline.com/run-chicken-run)
- Exposure times of 1/4 to 6 seconds per frame were supported by Aardman's own camera motors; typical exposure 1/2 to 1/4 s. [AC Aug 2000, p2](https://theasc.com/magazine/aug00/coop/pg2.htm), [Live Design](https://www.livedesignonline.com/run-chicken-run)
- Digital-era f-stops (Canon bodies, VMF or Dawn): **not found**; Riddett's piece gives no f-stop values. **[U]**
- Focus pulls are used on miniature sets in VMF, and soft backgrounds are a deliberate choice (Wrong Trousers backgrounds were mostly photocopied wallpaper because they are blurred; hand-painted detail appears only when the background comes into focus). [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=528137), [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/the-making-of-the-wrong-trousers-dream-teams)

### 5.3 Lighting for clay

- Workhorse lamp since The Wrong Trousers: Altman Micro Ellipse (small profile spot with shutters and gobo slot); mostly converted to LED, with ETC Source Four Mini as modern equivalent; ARRI 300/650 tungsten Fresnels, mostly 1K up to 5K for moon or daylight. Heavily diffused LED strip for large flat sets; Dragoneye LEDs for facial side-light; small LEDs on aluminium wire for backlight. [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)
- Dawn kept tungsten (Micro Ellipse plus gel choices) so the look matched the 2000 film, even though LEDs come close. [British Cinematographer (Copping)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/charles-copping-emchicken-run-dawn-of-the-nugget-em/)
- 2000-era kit: Strand 650 W to 10 kW Fresnels, 12 V 50 W Micro Ellipses, Kino Flo tubes for soft fill, mirrors 2 inches square to reach tight spaces, cucoloris for moon breakup; HMI avoided because of flicker on 2 to 6 second exposures. [Live Design](https://www.livedesignonline.com/run-chicken-run), [AC Oct 2005, p3](https://theasc.com/magazine/oct05/curse/page3.html)
- Lighting is treated as character: VMF drew on film noir and Hitchcock's Rebecca. [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/celebrating-the-inventiveness-of-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)
- Park: the Early Man puppets can be lit with the same drama as a live-action film. [Shoot](https://shootonline.com/?p=46923)
- Practical effects are lit in camera: a miniature projector for Norbot's numbers and Matrix-style code (chosen because it stays light- and colour-stable "for weeks"), gobos moved against each other for rippling cellar light. [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/celebrating-the-inventiveness-of-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/), [MPA interview](https://www.motionpictures.org/2025/01/wallace-and-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-directors-nick-park-merlin-crossingham-go-back-to-the-bakehouse/)

### 5.4 Flicker

- Consistent exposure per frame is "critically important"; Aardman built its own motors to hold exposure consistent. [AC Aug 2000, p2](https://theasc.com/magazine/aug00/coop/pg2.htm)
- Dimmer racks stabilise lamp output to prevent frame-to-frame voltage flicker; each mini-set has its own rack with a lamp-failure alarm. [Live Design](https://www.livedesignonline.com/run-chicken-run)
- Intentional flicker is engineered to be repeatable: Were-Rabbit candle flicker came from a stepper-driven disc with a wiggly line ("controllable and repeatable"), and in-frame candle bulbs were varied between 10 and 11 volts (later replaced with real flames in post). [AC Oct 2005, p3](https://theasc.com/magazine/oct05/curse/page3.html)
- Early Man used coloured jam jars to simulate fire flicker. [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett/)

### 5.5 Motion control and multiple exposures

- Dragonframe controls capture, lights and motion control; it can take multiple exposures per frame (key, ambient, "beauty" separately). Dawn used up to 16 exposures per frame; VMF did not, but alternate key lights helped the grade. [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)
- Mark Roberts Milo cranes (three on Dawn), Animoko rigs for basic 3D moves, custom heads and tracks. [British Cinematographer (Copping)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/charles-copping-emchicken-run-dawn-of-the-nugget-em/), [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)
- Aardman values the slightly bumpy look of old hand-pushed camera moves and now tries to emulate such "mistakes". [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett/)

### 5.6 VFX, by film

**Vengeance Most Fowl (2024)**
- Directors say there were "no CG characters", even for the Norbot army (cost and close-up quality); CG used where it "makes the film better". [Befores and Afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/01/17/a-mix-of-old-and-new/)
- Norbot army: a mechanised rig animated only four of 36 Norbots, plates shot at nine positions and stitched in post (Riddett); a separate account says 56 "evil" Norbots. **CONFLICT, probably different shots.** [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/), [Behind the Lens](https://behindthelensonline.net/site/interviews/interview-exclusives/nick-park-merlin-crossingham-talk-about-the-magic-of-making-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-exclusive-interview/)
- Water: canal water is CG (Houdini simulations), kept restrained and rounded to suit the handmade look; Park: live-action-looking water in a stop-motion world "doesn't work". Practical water uses hair gel, wax and Vaseline; splashes were animated frame by frame in clear plastic; poured liquid used animated crinkled cellophane; jam is clear resin. Milk begins as physical pouring and becomes CG when it hits Gromit; Wallace's wet look is applied to the puppet. [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=528137), [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/celebrating-the-inventiveness-of-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/), [Post Magazine](https://www.postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2024/November-December-2024/Animation-I-Wallace-Gromit-Vengeance-Most-Fowl-I.aspx), [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/), [Befores and Afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/01/17/a-mix-of-old-and-new/)

[Befores and Afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/01/17/a-mix-of-old-and-new/), [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=528137), [MPA interview](https://www.motionpictures.org/2025/01/wallace-and-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl-directors-nick-park-merlin-crossingham-go-back-to-the-bakehouse/)

### 5.7 Motion blur

- Historic: the Wrong Trousers, Close Shave, and Were-Rabbit chases were "always filmed with go motion", moving the camera or background during the exposure to blur the background. [AC Oct 2005, p5](https://theasc.com/magazine/oct05/curse/page5.html)

### 5.8 Grading

- Colourist Max Horton graded Were-Rabbit (2K, sequence-by-sequence) and VMF; the VMF boat chase took more grading time than any previous W&G film. [AC Oct 2005, p5](https://theasc.com/magazine/oct05/curse/page5.html), [British Cinematographer (Riddett)](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/dave-alex-riddett-bsc-wallace-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl/)

## 6. Implications for a CG clay pipeline (testable rules)

Rules are my recommendations built from the sources above. Numbers marked "ours" are proposals to tune, not Aardman figures.

**Pose cadence**
- R1. Default body and face pose cadence is twos: 12 unique poses per second at 24 fps. Test: for any default shot, count pose changes per second on all puppet meshes; expect 12, never 24, never in-between. (Post, MovieMaker.)
- R2. Camera and light changes run on ones when the camera moves. Test: camera transform differs on 24 of 24 frames in moving shots. (Post.)
- R3. When ones camera moves run under twos animation, put the pose change on alternate frames and let the camera interpolate, i.e. the "one-and-a-half" case. Test: no frame shows both puppet and camera at a held pair identical to the previous frame during a move. (Post.) **[U]** on exact implementation.
- R4. Allow a `singles` flag per shot for fast action and complex camera moves, defaulting off. (Animation Magazine on Farmageddon.)
- R5. Any CG background crowd, birds or digital doubles share the same twos cadence as the puppets. Test: pose-change frames of background rigs equal those of foreground puppets in the same shot. (Skwigly.)

**Faces**
- R6. Mouths are discrete swaps from a library, not blend shapes. Per character: 14 default, up to 24 for a hero speaker; Early Man reached more than 24. Test: each frame's mouth is exactly one library mesh; no vertex-weight tweening. (Dawn, Early Man.)
- R7. Hand-blended seam between mouth insert and face: add a slightly irregular clay seam rather than a hard-edged cut. (Riddett on blending.) **[U]** on seam look.
- R8. Eyes move by discrete steps; blink or eye change is a swap held for 2 frames minimum (Ginger's eyes swap every two frames). Gromit has no eyelid animation: brows and eye direction only. Test: Gromit rig has zero lid keys. (Dawn, Cartoon Brew Connect.)
- R9. Mouth timing on single frames is not supported by any source; keep mouths on twos by default, allow ones for plosives as a flagged exception. **[U]**

**Surface, fingerprints, boil**
- R10. Fingerprints and tool marks appear on faces as well as bodies and costume. Place them at brow, forehead, cheeks and hands, and on silicone-class parts such as sleeves. (Gromit forehead, Fell, Cox, Crossingham.) Prints on clay parts are re-seeded per pose; prints on silicone-class parts are static per puppet (cast from the sculpt). (Fandom, MPA.) Static versus per-pose is **[U]** inference.
- R11. Ridge spacing 0.41 to 0.46 mm at puppet scale is physically consistent with a real animator fingertip on a 15 to 18 cm puppet. **[U]** No Aardman source gives a number. Keep it; on a puppet 17 cm tall it reads as a very large, soft-edged print at human scale.
- R12. Surface boil is per-material: clay skin low amplitude, re-seeded only on pose-change frames; hair and fur may twitch (Early Man style) behind a `hair_boil` flag; stiffened-fleece style fur gets zero boil; cast silicone zero boil. (P&J, Cartoon Brew interview.)
- R13. Colour is constant per character across a whole film; micro-detail is not. Test: per-character base albedo identical between shots (delta E under 1 in our units); surface detail seeds change at every shot cut because heads and mouths are cleaned or swapped between shots. (Kottke on frame 1 vs 6800; CS Monitor, Shoot.)
- R14. Dust and lint: sparse, low-contrast specks that are more visible on pale clays and change per shot (cleaned heads, baby wipes). (Shoot, AWN.) Test: dust count per face region is below a cap (ours) and regenerates per shot.
- R15. Matte first: clay and silicone share one low-sheen microfacet response; add an optional "sugar dust" micro-sheen layer for chicken-style fine matte. Test: render clay and silicone patches under white, blue, warm light and require luminance within a small band (ours) because Riddett tested exactly this. (Riddett, Dawn.)
- R16. Skin must not clip. Test: no more than 0.1 percent of skin pixels at display white. (AC Oct 2005, p2: "only so many ways to light a piece of Plasticine"; "exposed and overexposed" gap is small.)

**Timing and output budget (for a time-per-shot planner)**
- R17. Use 4 to 5 seconds per animator-week as the planning figure for hero shots, and about 1 second per day for complex shots. Heavy shots run about 0.4 s/day (80 working days for 32.67 s on Dawn). (Post, Animation Magazine, TVBEurope.)

**Camera, lens, light**
- R18. Camera model: full-frame sensor **[U]**, close-focus primes; default 24 mm world lens, 18 to 20 mm for close puppet shots, 14 mm to 300 mm allowed. (AC Oct 2005, p2 and p3; Riddett.)
- R19. Depth of field: deep focus on the puppet is the default; use stopped-down apertures, with an upper extreme near T22 (about f/20) for macro-close shots. Keep f/11 as the standard and allow f/16 to f/22 on close shots. Allow background fall-off by set depth and focus pulls as a creative tool. (AC Oct 2005, p3; Live Design; fxguide.) The f/11 default is **[U]**; no source states it.
- R20. Lighting: tungsten-like profile spots with gobos and shutters (Micro Ellipse model), warm key, blue moon with green-screen-safe separation, small hard pools of light, mirrors or small LEDs for rim. (Riddett, Copping, Live Design.)
- R21. Render light passes the way Dragonframe shoots multi-exposure: key, ambient and beauty as separate layers; allow up to 16 passes per frame for Dawn-style grading. (Riddett.)

**Flicker and motion blur**
- R22. Global flicker stays near zero. Test: mean luminance of static background pixels varies under 0.5 percent frame to frame (ours). (AC Aug 2000, Live Design.)
- R23. Exception: fire and candle practicals get deliberate, deterministic, repeatable flicker, e.g. 10 to 11 volt-equivalent variation (about 10 percent) on the local light only. (AC Oct 2005, p3.)
- R24. Default: zero motion blur on puppets. Exception: background-only "go motion" blur for chase shots when the moving background or camera travels about 7 to 10 cm per frame at model scale (Wrong Trousers: 3 inches per frame camera, 10 cm per frame train). The threshold is ours **[U]**. Fog and mist elements run at 24 Hz independent of the 12 Hz puppet poses. (AC Oct 2005, p2 and p5; Inverse; Riddett.)

**Materials per body part (default mapping)**
- R25. Heads, hands, brows and mouths: plasticine shader with boil. Bodies and costume: silicone-class (smooth, matte, cast fingerprints). Gromit stretch shots: whole body clay. Wallace's vest: hard plastic, no boil. (VMF, Science and Media Museum.)
- R26. Liquids and effects: simulate restrained, rounded water; tears and drops as gel beads moved in steps; fire as practical-style flicker, not photoreal; keep water sim cadence on twos. (VMF, Animation Magazine.) Cadence on twos for liquids is **[U]**.

## 8. Source conflicts and open questions (summary)

1. Newplast base: air-drying (Oxford Times) vs wax-based (WSJ summary) vs oil-based, non-drying (HUE). Use HUE.
2. Clay stock value: two years / one film / half a feature. Aardman says no problem.
3. Output per animator: 1 to 5 s/day vs 3.5 to 5 s/week depending on film and outlet; Cultbox's "1-5 seconds a day" for VMF looks like a typo.
4. Dawn VFX shot counts: 1,400 / 28 CG (TVBEurope, Animation Magazine) vs 1,500 / 20 (Variety).
5. Ginger puppet counts on Dawn: 18 vs 16.
6. Early Man puppet material: Shoot calls them silicone bodies with clay faces and also "plasticine"; treat as silicone body plus clay face.
7. Norbot army: 4 of 36 animated (Riddett) vs 56 (Behind the Lens).
8. Farmageddon scale: "centimetres high" (Scotsman) vs Shaun 17 cm.
9. Not found: Wallace mouth count; alcohol cleaning; digital-era f-stops; Morph's current construction; colour-mixing procedure; whether Aardman now buys from HUE (secondary only).
