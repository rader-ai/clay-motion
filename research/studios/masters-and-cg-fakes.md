# Masters of handmade stop motion, and CG that imitated it

Research for the AI stop-motion clay studio (Blender, Cycles, everything rendered from code). Compiled 2026-10-17.

## How to read this report

- Every factual line ends with a source URL in angle brackets. **[U]** marks a line that is unverified, inferred, vendor-only, uncited by its own author, taken from a forum or Wikipedia, or my own calculation.
- Method limits. Pages were read through a fetch tool that returns condensed summaries, so figures are "as reported there" and were not re-read in the original page. Some pages were paywalled or truncated (IBC Pinocchio feature, Post Magazine, the Aardman podcast pages, part of the fxguide "brick-by-brick" piece). The search quota ran out near the end of the run, so the follow-ups listed under "Gaps" were not done.
- Quotes are short phrases only. Everything else is paraphrased.
- Where sources disagree, both are given.

# PART A. Other masters: what makes the work read as handmade

## A1. Adam Elliot (Harvie Krumpet 2003, Mary and Max 2009, Memoir of a Snail 2024)

**Stated views**
- Elliot says the fingerprints in the clay are deliberately kept, as proof the characters are "tangible, tactile things" and real. <https://ca.news.yahoo.com/adam-elliot-talks-sarah-snook-220514094.html>
- He describes the puppets as "very lumpy and asymmetrical" and wants to celebrate the flaws. He frames visible fingerprints as celebrating the tangible nature of the puppets. <https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/audience-will-forgive-bad-animating-theyll-never-forgive-bad-story-adam-elliot-memoir-snail>
- He wanted the models to look imperfect and to show brush strokes and the process of creation. On small errors in skin and hair his attitude was "90% fine ... that'll do". <https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/living-forward-adam-elliot-on-memoir-of-a-snail>
- He tells animators they are the actors and welcomes "happy accidents", for example when a stiff armature forced a change mid-shot. <https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/living-forward-adam-elliot-on-memoir-of-a-snail>
- On digital work he said "no CGI", even the fire is yellow cellophane, clouds are hand-painted, and fire was done in camera. <https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/living-forward-adam-elliot-on-memoir-of-a-snail>
- He also said "we're drowning in CGI animation", and ties renewed interest in stop motion partly to that and to AI. <https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/audience-will-forgive-bad-animating-theyll-never-forgive-bad-story-adam-elliot-memoir-snail> and <https://ca.news.yahoo.com/adam-elliot-talks-sarah-snook-220514094.html>
- He is not a digital purist: LED lights and digital cameras were used ("we certainly embrace new technology"). <https://ca.news.yahoo.com/adam-elliot-talks-sarah-snook-220514094.html>
- Fire, water and smoke are the hard parts; he admits fire "could have easily" been done digitally and they chose not to. <https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/audience-will-forgive-bad-animating-theyll-never-forgive-bad-story-adam-elliot-memoir-snail>
- His tremor is built into the look: uneven lines, an organic feel. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Elliot> [U, Wikipedia]
- For Harvie Krumpet he left fingerprints in the plasticine on purpose so the audience would read the film as tangible rather than computer-generated. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvie_Krumpet> [U, Wikipedia]

**Materials and look**
- Memoir's puppets were made from Apoxie Sculpt, which cures hard and rigid. Costumes were sculpted and painted on, not sewn. The crew wanted the "lumps and bumps, brushstrokes, and fingerprints" to stay visible. <https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13144738>
- Another source gives the four main ingredients as clay, wire, paper and paint, with props hand-painted rather than airbrushed. <https://animationmagazine.net/?p=3258464>
- Elliot skipped fabric because it is "too finicky". <https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/living-forward-adam-elliot-on-memoir-of-a-snail>
- Palette (Memoir). Critics describe a grey, overcast, drab Australia and call the look "chunky wonky" with nothing perfectly straight. <https://theconversation.com/overtly-handmade-and-so-very-moving-adam-elliots-memoir-of-a-snail-is-a-stop-motion-triumph-233105> Another review says skin tones are pallid with browns and greys dominating. <https://www.theskinny.co.uk/film/new-releases/memoir-of-a-snail> I found no palette statement from Elliot himself.
- Palette (Mary and Max). The National Film and Sound Archive describes a monochrome tonality, warmer in Melbourne, starker in New York, with red accents (lipstick, a pompom). <https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/mary-and-max-two-maxs-life-goals-come-true> The LA Times is quoted as describing noir blacks, whites and greys for Max and sepia suburbia for Mary. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_and_Max> [U, Wikipedia relaying LA Times]
- Palette (early shorts). Uncle and Cousin use greyscale, Ernie Biscuit is black and white. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Elliot> [U, Wikipedia]

**Numbers**
- Memoir of a Snail. Seven animators. Shoot 33 weeks. Each animator made about 5 to 10 seconds per day, roughly one shot a day. About 200 sets, 200 characters, roughly 7,000 snails. <https://www.austinchronicle.com/screens/how-memoir-of-a-snail-came-out-of-its-shell-13286572/>
- Shoot length conflict: Elliot himself says 32 weeks. <https://ca.news.yahoo.com/adam-elliot-talks-sarah-snook-220514094.html> Cartoon Brew also says 32 weeks and about 10 seconds per animator per day. <https://cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/how-an-animators-dismembered-fingertip-ended-up-in-adam-elliots-memoir-of-a-snail-243510.html>
- Production scale as reported by VFX Voice: nine stages, about 200 sets, about 200 characters, 5,000 to 7,000 props, about 600 VFX shots (mostly simple 2D in Nuke or Fusion), church fire up to 15 layers, greenscreen only for a few skies and car plates. <https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13144738>
- Digital was used for cleanup (rig removal, painting out reflections) and the shoot used Dragonframe; the film has no CG added to what the camera saw. <https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13144738>
- Elliot uses voice-over narration because lip sync is so tight and the film has "no walking". <https://ca.news.yahoo.com/adam-elliot-talks-sarah-snook-220514094.html>
- Oscar-nominated Best Animated Feature (97th Academy Awards); budget reported as US$4.5M. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memoir_of_a_Snail> [U, Wikipedia]
- Mary and Max. 57 weeks of principal photography, 133 sets, 212 puppets, 475 props; six animation teams in a converted factory. <https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/mary-and-max-two-maxs-life-goals-come-true> (The same page quotes a crew-average output that does not reconcile with the 57-week shoot, so I am not using it. [U])
- Harvie Krumpet. About 15 months of filming, Elliot plus two assistants, about 3 to 5 seconds of film per full day, shot on Super 16 mm with a Bolex, models roughly wine-bottle size, more than 30 mouth shapes for Harvie. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvie_Krumpet> [U, Wikipedia]
- Frame rate: I found no statement of Elliot's cadence (ones, twos or threes). A museum exhibit page says stop motion runs at 24 fps, which is generic, not Elliot-specific. <https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/memoir-snail-props-sets-characters-adam-elliot/> The 5 to 10 seconds per animator per day figure is several times the Laika rate (see A5), which suggests sparse, held, low-density animation (twos or slower, narration-driven). [U, inference]

## A2. Henry Selick (Wendell and Wild 2022, Coraline 2009, The Nightmare Before Christmas 1993)

- Selick felt stop motion had become so slick and perfect that it was indistinguishable from CG, so on Wendell and Wild he chose to pull back from that and "leave more mistakes in". <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/09/07/i-wanted-to-pull-back-from-that-sort-of-perfection-and-leave-more-mistakes-in/>
- Frame rate: he says performance still works at 12 fps "and sometimes even less", and the film mixes twos and sometimes threes instead of shooting everything on ones. <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/09/07/i-wanted-to-pull-back-from-that-sort-of-perfection-and-leave-more-mistakes-in/>
- In a second interview: they shoot 24 fps but "often mix in twos"; it "may stutter a slight amount", and he likes that. <https://www.awn.com/print/animationworld/constructing-wendell-wild-conversation-legendary-henry-selick>
- BFI's Queline Meadows says Selick shot Wendell and Wild on twos and threes to avoid an overly smooth look. <https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/stop-motion-imperfection-why-animation-needs-human-touch> (Meadows is a critic relaying this; Selick's own words are above.)
- Seams between replacement-face pieces: on Coraline he wanted them visible, was overruled, and they were painted out. On Wendell and Wild he kept them: "I did not want to paint them out in post". <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/09/07/i-wanted-to-pull-back-from-that-sort-of-perfection-and-leave-more-mistakes-in/>
- He believes viewers stop noticing seams after a few minutes. <https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/stop-motion-henry-selick-coraline>
- On Coraline's face split he said leaving the line in "lets you know that this is a hand made movie". Also: "I like to see the hand of an artist." And: "I don't like airbrushed photorealism". <https://www.focusfeatures.com/article/henry_selick_in_conversation>
- Make flaws part of the pitch: "the charm of stop-motion", "perfectionism with a squinted eye", and "just do it CG if it's going to look like CG". <https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/henry-selick-directors-cut> (Cartoon Brew gives a near-identical sentiment: if a CG look is the goal, "just make it computer animation". <https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/henry-selick-wendell-and-wild-interview-222428.html>)
- Cleanup policy on Wendell and Wild: "leave in the minor flaws", but rigs get a clean pass and are painted out. <https://www.awn.com/print/animationworld/constructing-wendell-wild-conversation-legendary-henry-selick>
- VFX Voice: 1,568 VFX shots across about ten vendors plus in-house; rigs removed digitally; seams "left in all of the shots" and cleaned only when distracting; CG snow, fog, matte paintings, set extensions, rain on the car; shadow puppets fully CG; physical sets, puppets, miniatures, light rods for lightning; motion-control background passes. <https://vfxvoice.com/enhancing-the-practical-mantra-of-wendell-wild/>
- Staffing and pace: usually one animator per shot; a 2 to 10 second shot takes four days to two weeks. <https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/henry-selick-directors-cut>
- Good frame grabs let animators repair mistakes by reshooting, so mistakes "not a disaster". <https://www.awn.com/print/animationworld/constructing-wendell-wild-conversation-legendary-henry-selick>

## A3. Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio (2022, ShadowMachine, Mark Gustafson co-director)

**Philosophy and imperfection**
- Del Toro's description of the world: "perfectly imperfect". Production designer Guy Davis: "a sense of realism but not reality". <https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/making-of-pinocchio-guillermo-del-toro-1235271734/>
- Pinocchio is deliberately unfinished: carved by someone "drunk at the time", so "deeply imperfect". Del Toro wants kids to see animation "can be handmade". Sets are hand carved, painted and "lit by real light". <https://newsletter.oscars.org/features/guillermo-del-toro-mark-gustafson-pinocchio-interview>
- Pinocchio's face is asymmetrical, one side fully sculpted, the other left unfinished. <https://blog.frame.io/2022/12/19/guillermo-del-toro-pinocchio-animation-behind-the-scenes/>
- Gustafson calls himself "not a technical perfectionist", says perfection of technique is not worth chasing too far, and wants animators to have agency. They kept things in camera where possible, but almost all skies are digital because there was no room to paint them. <https://bfi.org.uk/interviews/guillermo-del-toro-pinocchio-stop-motion-mark-gustafson>
- Painted surfaces use an illustrative realism (Wyeth, Rockwell references) to unify characters made of different materials. <https://blog.frame.io/2022/12/19/guillermo-del-toro-pinocchio-animation-behind-the-scenes/>
- Pinocchio puppet is about 9.5 inches, uses replacement animation (printed head) so wood grain shows; most other leads use mechanical silicone faces. <https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/making-of-pinocchio-guillermo-del-toro-1235271734/> and <https://vfxvoice.com/stop-motion-breathes-new-life-into-guillermo-del-toros-pinocchio/>

**Output and scale**
- Roughly three years of production; as many as 60 small stages running at once. <https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/making-of-pinocchio-guillermo-del-toro-1235271734/>
- Planned 34 lighting units for 17 animators; four lighting camera operators and three motion-control operators; the crew needed more lights than planned. <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/12/14/how-does-cinematography-work-on-a-stop-motion-film/> (Stage-count and unit-count figures differ between sources, 17 animators with 34 units versus up to 60 stages; they may describe different moments.)
- 1,374 VFX shots, mainly by MR. X with BOT VFX. <https://vfxvoice.com/stop-motion-breathes-new-life-into-guillermo-del-toros-pinocchio/> Before-and-afters attributes the sims, digital environments and all skies to MPC. <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/> (Vendor attribution conflicts; both are reported.)

**Camera, lighting, timing**
- Canon 5D chosen over mirrorless because of dust (some shots ran up to three weeks). Lenses from 15 mm (Laowa) up; Zeiss for close focus around 25 mm; Nikon for most others. del Toro's live action sits at 28 to 85 mm, animation needed wider. <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/12/14/how-does-cinematography-work-on-a-stop-motion-film/>
- Strobing: shooting in 4K raised the risk of strobing on lateral tracking shots, so animators who normally shot on twos shot those camera moves on ones. <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/12/14/how-does-cinematography-work-on-a-stop-motion-film/>
- The same article says the opening key light moves roughly 7 to 8 feet on set over about 10 seconds to read as a sunrise, with light movement meant to be "felt" more than seen. <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/12/14/how-does-cinematography-work-on-a-stop-motion-film/>
- Tungsten keys (some converted to LED), LED fill and bounce. <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/12/14/how-does-cinematography-work-on-a-stop-motion-film/>
- Cinematographer Frank Passingham describes "layered lighting": multiple exposures per frame combined in VFX (for example warm sun on Pinocchio above water, green light below). A flashlight was shot in a single pass using small LEDs rather than an animated light. <https://pastemagazine.com/movies/guillermo-del-toro/guillermo-del-toros-pinocchio-frank-passingham-cinematographer-interview>
- Sets were kept grounded, "no whacky angles", so Pinocchio is the most fantastical thing in frame. <https://bfi.org.uk/interviews/guillermo-del-toro-pinocchio-stop-motion-mark-gustafson>
- Stop motion in Pinocchio was not always on twos; frame rates varied to keep motion smooth where needed. <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/>
- A general explainer says 24 or 30 fps is usually shot "on twos", meaning 12 to 15 distinct poses per second. <https://blog.frame.io/2022/12/19/guillermo-del-toro-pinocchio-animation-behind-the-scenes/> (General context, not a Pinocchio-specific figure.)

**VFX layering, matched to handmade**
- Principle: shoot or build whatever is possible, VFX as fallback; effects should look "as if they could have shot it". <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/>
- Water: Houdini FLIP sims were post-processed to strip lateral flow so the surface moves only vertically, like a rubber sheet, plus a crystalline froth layer. Rain drops were modelled on the on-set glycerin drops. Fire used a cloth sim based on a cheesecloth flicker test, lit with a repeatable flicker rig. Explosions and clouds were modelled on stop-motion cotton. Skies were based on classical painting with visible brush strokes. <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/>
- About 38 to 40 unique skies, each keyframe painted; del Toro pointed to Grant Wood, Georgia O'Keeffe and the Group of Seven; clouds designed to look handmade and slightly artificial. Water landscapes were art-directed to look "artificial". <https://vfxvoice.com/stop-motion-breathes-new-life-into-guillermo-del-toros-pinocchio/>
- A "charm debate" decided which artifacts to keep or fix; some face chatter was cleaned, some deliberately kept. <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/>
- Nearly every shot was stabilized because wood sets moved with temperature; light flicker was a concern; dust and movement distractions were removed while keeping as much stop-motion character as possible. <https://vfxvoice.com/stop-motion-breathes-new-life-into-guillermo-del-toros-pinocchio/>
- Rig removal: a hero take, then puppets removed and a clean plate shot; set shifts from studio moisture and rig shadows were recurring problems. <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/>
- Stop-start puppet motion caused problems for smooth-biased simulations; the team used in-betweens and workarounds. <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/>
- Digital doubles were used for crowds, not for hero performances. <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/>

## A4. Wes Anderson (Fantastic Mr. Fox 2009, Isle of Dogs 2018)

**Fantastic Mr. Fox**
- Characters were animated on twos, 12 positions per second. <https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/Web-Exclusives/2010/Smart-as-a-Fox.aspx>
- Anderson wanted the fur boil: animator Andy Biddle says Wes "wanted the boiling". Animators kept fur moving by blowing on it or brushing hair on an eyebrow to hold an expression, after first trying hairspray and gel to control it. Goat hair was used because synthetic fur lacked quality. <https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/fantastic-mr-fox-animators-interview-ian-mckinnon-andy-biddle-and-andy-ghent/>
- Anderson on fur: he loves puppets with fur, and he says there is "nothing you can do about" the invisible-wind effect of fingers on fur. He wanted a "handmade feeling", fun and upbeat rather than perfect, and a "Coraline level of precision" would have cost far more. <https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/wes-anderson-talks-fantastic-mr-fox-rt-interview/>
- Anderson wanted an older, less polished stop-motion look and pushed animators out of their comfort zone, referencing 1930s films; he banned blinking. <https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/fantastic-mr-fox-animators-interview-ian-mckinnon-andy-biddle-and-andy-ghent/>
- Starevich's The Tale of the Fox (twitchy fur) is cited as an inspiration. <https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/stop-motion-imperfection-why-animation-needs-human-touch>
- Scales and counts: six modelling scales, about 530 puppets, smallest puppets thumbnail-sized, hero Mr Fox roughly the size of Mr Bean. Small single-wire puppets broke often; Biddle estimated one every 14 frames. Biddle puts output at about 4 seconds of footage a day. <https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/fantastic-mr-fox-animators-interview-ian-mckinnon-andy-biddle-and-andy-ghent/>
- Practical effects: smoke is cotton wool, grass is towelling, flames carved from orange soap, candle flames animated against black and composited. <https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/wes-anderson-talks-fantastic-mr-fox-rt-interview/> and <https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/Web-Exclusives/2010/Smart-as-a-Fox.aspx>
- CG used sparingly: leaves, dirt, crumbs, set extensions, rig removal, CG chickens, compositing. When simulations were proposed Anderson often said no. A VFX artist noted the computer "doesn't like to hold a simulation for two frames". <https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/Web-Exclusives/2010/Smart-as-a-Fox.aspx>

**Isle of Dogs**
- Animators shot predominantly on twos, 12 images per second, to get a look with more edge than perfectly smooth stop motion. <https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/isle-dogs-animation-director-mark-waring-explains-animate-wes-anderson-film-157358.html>
- The natural boil was kept; hairspray and gel stiffened fur clumps so fur shimmers slightly even when a puppet is still. <https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/isle-dogs-animation-director-mark-waring-explains-animate-wes-anderson-film-157358.html>
- AWN version: Waring says "you have to almost animate the fur frame by frame", with clumps moved so the fur looks influenced by an outside force. <https://awn.com/animationworld/wes-andersons-isle-dogs-gives-erudite-canines-their-day>
- Anderson wanted viewers "definitely watching a stop-frame film and not a cg film"; symmetrical composition. <https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/isle-dogs-animation-director-mark-waring-explains-animate-wes-anderson-film-157358.html>
- Puppets and crew: about 1,000 puppets (500 human, 500 dog), about 240 sets, around 30 animators and assistants shooting at once (Cartoon Brew); AWN says about 27 animators plus 10 assistants and as many as 50 shooting units; the ASC says up to 44 units at peak on 3 Mills stages and about 87 weeks of animation. <https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/isle-dogs-animation-director-mark-waring-explains-animate-wes-anderson-film-157358.html> <https://awn.com/animationworld/wes-andersons-isle-dogs-gives-erudite-canines-their-day> <https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/> (Unit counts differ by source.)
- Scale: "many scales", but most of the movie shot in one scale; dogs at half size "started to look rather puppyish". <https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/>
- Camera: Canon 1D X, about 150 old Nikon manual primes, default 20 mm and most close-ups on 28 mm. <https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/>
- Light: "white and nearly always quite flat", exteriors with almost no key. Lighting effects were captured as 16 variants of each frame. <https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/> Exteriors keyed with a large 10K source bounced or diffused; size of the light matters more than intensity. <https://filmmakermagazine.com/?p=105130>
- Exposure and aperture: Oliver winds the aperture "right down to the bottom" and still falls short on depth; exposures typically a quarter second to two seconds, up to four; ISO 100. When depth is insufficient the team shoots planes separately and composites. <https://filmmakermagazine.com/?p=105130> The ASC piece confirms the plane-splitting workaround when zooms lost depth of field. <https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/>
- Oliver: Anderson likes very wide lenses "with as much depth of field as he can get"; matching Anderson's live-action depth of field is "essentially impossible" at miniature scale; lighting is "super flat". <https://cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/isle-of-dogs-cinematography-tristan-oliver-157383.html>
- Fur close-ups were shot twice (with and without greenscreen) so hair detail and matte could be separated. <https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/>

## A5. Other makers worth knowing (Aardman, Laika, indie and European work, 2025 to 2026)

**Aardman**
- Wallace and Gromit: Vengeance Most Fowl (Dec 2024). Bodies are silicone, not foam latex, and the silicone is "full of fingerprints"; the switch saves time on joint cleanup. Norbot heads and mechanisms are 3D printed. Milk and water surface are CG hybrids. No CG characters; the Norbot army was shot with real puppets. <https://beforesandafters.com/2025/01/17/a-mix-of-old-and-new/>
- Will Becher (Aardman supervising animator): stop motion cannot be repeated exactly, "the lens and the lighting, everything is organic". A test that cloned one row of Norbots from different camera positions failed because "the lighting doesn't change". And: "we won't do stuff in camera for the sake of it". <https://beforesandafters.com/2025/01/17/a-mix-of-old-and-new/>
- A review notes visible fingerprints in the clay are often seen and that some viewers may read them as flaws. <https://moviejawn.com/home/2024/12/18/wallace-and-gromit-vengeance-most-fowl>
- Aardman and AI. Sarah Cox: "While we don't use Generative AI to produce content", and she says there is strong desire to see human input and real materials. <https://www.c21media.net/perspective/liberating-or-limiting-aardmans-sarah-cox-on-ai-in-animation/> Shortlist attributes to Cox "We don't use generative AI in our films" and to managing director Sean Clarke "the hand of the maker, sometimes literally in the fingerprints in the clay". <https://www.shortlist.com/entertainment/aardmans-sir-peter-lord-on-his-journey-from-a-kitchen-table-to-hollywood-rocket-packs-and-how-the-studio-favours-thumbiness-over-ai> Conflicting posture: Nick Park says it is "too easy to create rubbish", while Aardman said it would "embrace" AI cautiously. <https://www.aol.com/articles/wallace-gromit-creators-share-stance-095029074.html>
- Robin Robin (2021, Aardman, Netflix). Needle-felt and fuzzy felt, chosen as sculptable "like plasticine"; strong backlight makes felt glow (rim light). Fibre boil was kept restrained ("quite clean"), with looser boil tested and reserved for intimate scenes. Shot partly in stereo to get depth maps so real stop-motion mist, snow and water could be integrated. Not CG. <https://www.cartoonbrew.com/stop-motion/the-directors-of-robin-robin-explain-how-they-broke-with-aardmans-house-style-209457.html>
- Shaun the Sheep: The Beast of Mossy Bottom. US theatrical release Sept 18, 2026 (GKIDS), UK Sept 18 (Sky), directors Steve Cox and Matthew Walker. <https://gkids.com/?p=6621>
- Aardman's Pokemon Tales stop-motion series (2027) was announced at Annecy in June 2026. <https://www.creativebloq.com/art/animation/hooray-pokemon-is-fighting-back-against-ai-slop-and-backing-the-handmade-approach>

**Laika**
- Kubo averaged slightly under 3.5 seconds per animator per week; target is 5 to 6; principal animation just over 90 weeks. <https://www.stlmag.com/news/q-and-a-with-brad-schiff>
- Cloth and hair have no simulation in stop motion; baggy costumes were "a frickin' nightmare" to control. <https://www.stlmag.com/news/q-and-a-with-brad-schiff>

# PART B. CG made to look like stop motion

## B1. The Lego films (Animal Logic)

**The Lego Movie (2014)**
- The team animated "on twos and threes", used no motion blur (a first for the studio), and built a "brick blur", a smear of character-coloured bricks replacing the character for fast motion. <https://www.motionpictures.org/2014/02/how-the-lego-movie-got-its-stop-motion-look/>
- 3D World adds that animators began on twos, then used threes or fours for fast action to get the rougher feel of brick-film shorts, because CG tends to look "too smooth"; no joint bending, no squash and stretch; brick blur took months to develop. <https://www.pressreader.com/australia/3d-world/20140520/282961038158328>
- Physical rules: arms and legs do not bend; splits hide the seam; clapping is staged to avoid visible arm intersection; faces are a library of 2D expressions applied like sticker replacement, with small bumps and scratches that imitate wear. <https://www.motionpictures.org/2014/02/how-the-lego-movie-got-its-stop-motion-look/>
- Directors' rule: any frame could be frozen and built by hand. Phil Lord said they researched "how to get fingerprint smudges onto the LEGO bricks", most visible on Emmet's shiny plastic. <https://www.lego.com/categories/adults-welcome/article/was-the-lego-movie-stop-motion>
- Wear data: over 2,000 individual bricks surfaced with scratches, fingerprints and imperfections, informed by macro photography of real bricks; handheld-style virtual Steadicam rigs, keyframed. <https://animallogic.com/portfolio/projects/tlm/>
- Procedural wear: semi-procedural scratches, stud detail and edge wear; baked occlusion for grunge; a small random transform jitter so loosely built models do not align perfectly. <https://animallogic.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/The-LEGO-Movie-Bricks-Bricks-and-More-Bricks.pdf>
- Minifigures were examined under microscopes for seams, dirt and grime; some outdoor sets were weathered with resin treatments seen at LEGOLAND. <https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/brick-by-brick-how-animal-logic-crafted-the-lego-movie/>
- Motion blur nuance: fxguide says a little camera blur was used sparingly when the art director wanted it. <https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/brick-by-brick-how-animal-logic-crafted-the-lego-movie/>
- Depth of field. Real lens proxies were kept so DoF could be toggled and shots framed first; the camera was meant to feel handheld and a bit nervous, not a single-axis CG float. <https://www.fxguide.com/?p=48503>
- VFX Voice: a workflow for character animation without motion blur; dust and physical smoke were largely dropped to return to the traditional stop-motion look; dirt scattered semi-procedurally, collecting in corners; explosions sculpted by hand and triggered with replacement animation, sculpting "each frame of the brick". <https://vfxvoice.com/the-building-blocks-behind-the-lego-movie-2/>

**The Lego Batman Movie (2017)**
- Rob Coleman (head of animation) called the approach "emulated stop-motion"; director Chris McKay kept the stop-motion aesthetic while loosening some Lego rules for expression; artists got a lens kit adding stray light, flare and dirty lenses. <https://animallogic.com/portfolio/projects/tlbm-2/>
- No squash and stretch; bends are done by swapping digital Lego pieces; when Batman drives a second pair of arms is swapped in. The dolphins were animated "often on 2s and with no motion blur". <https://www.fxguide.com/?p=189960>
- Water exception: "in a stop frame animated film there would be no motion blur", but real stop-frame water is impossible; with blur off the splashes looked wrong, so partial motion blur was added to water. <https://www.fxguide.com/?p=189960>
- Depth of field: the films are treated as shot by a Lego-sized crew with a tiny 50 mm lens, so focus falls off quickly at 1.5 inch figure scale. Glimpse renders physically accurate DoF and bokeh from nonlinear lens models. (fxguide uses "DOF" to mean shallow focus.) <https://www.fxguide.com/?p=189960>
- Brick unique-ID drives lookups into scratch maps, thumb print maps, dent maps and edge maps so no two bricks match. <https://www.fxguide.com/?p=189960>
- Effects rule: if Nolan could do it with miniatures, do it. Brick-built smoke was tried and dropped because it did not read as smoke. Water scale tests used real sinks; physically "accurate" scales sometimes looked wrong. References studied included Thunderbirds and Fantastic Mr Fox. <https://www.fxguide.com/?p=189960>
- Animal Logic page: a jitter algorithm keeps brick structures from aligning perfectly; 81,854 textures including scratches, dirt and fingerprints. <https://animallogic.com/portfolio/projects/tlbm-2/>

**The Lego Movie 2 (2019)**
- Animation Supervisor Dave Burgess: characters are usually animated on 2s "to help with the handmade stop motion feel"; live-action sequences were put on 1s so the minifig felt like a handheld toy, and "lots of random noise" was added. <https://animallogic.com/portfolio/projects/the-lego-movie-2/>
- Explosions were hand built rather than simulated; effects were physically tested in the real world first. <https://animallogic.com/portfolio/projects/the-lego-movie-2/>
- Appreciation piece: the style is "naive, imperfect", staccato with held poses; McKay is a stop-motion veteran. <https://www.cartoonbrew.com/cgi/lets-talk-about-the-animation-in-the-lego-movie-95781.html>

## B2. Other CG that deliberately imitated stop motion or clay

- **The Willoughbys (2020, Netflix).** Director Kris Pearn: "largely on twos and every now and then we break down to threes"; fire, candles and snow often on threes or fours; "very, very little motion blur", only to hide things like pans; no blur reinforced the miniature feel; textures "from going to a craft store"; a slight off-set and looseness to everything. <https://beforesandafters.com/2020/04/22/director-kris-pearn-on-the-heightened-style-of-the-willoughbys/> Cartoon Brew adds heightened textures, "nothing should feel digital or crisp", a simple camera, and strong depth of field. <https://cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/how-the-willoughbys-uses-cgi-to-create-a-handmade-look-exclusive-art-189892.html> ("Strong DoF" here is ambiguous between shallow and deep. That seemed a conceit." <https://cgw.com/Publications/CGW/2006/Volume-29-Issue-10-Oct-2006-/Flushed-with-Success.aspx> Co-director David Bowers, in contrast, said the team "toyed with putting fingerprints into the film" and a viewer might find a thumbprint on a nose. <https://popmatters.com/an-imperfect-world-interview-with-sam-fell-and-david-bowers-directors-of-fl-2495740124.html> (Internal conflict, both reported.) - **Something's Awry (Kris Theorin).** Adds little notches to paper props so they look not cut perfectly straight, tapers furniture, adds warmth via photoreal look. <https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2023/the-geometry-of-movement-computer-generated-imagery-in-film/from-legos-to-cgi-an-interview-with-kris-theorin/>
- **Aaron Ordover (Blender).** Fakes cut-paper stop motion with flat layers, visible borders, volumetric lighting; admits it is "still digital". <https://aaronordover.substack.com/p/fake-stop-motion-easy-mode> [U, hobbyist]
- **Leads only (TV Tropes list; not verified):** Pingu in the City, Big Nate (CG at 12 fps), Go! Go! Cory Carson, Remy and Boo, Minions' bedtime segment, Lightyear's go-motion style robots. <https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StopFauxtion> [U]

- **Puss in Boots: The Last Wish.** Director Joel Crawford says during the rooftop running "the motion isn't always super-smooth" on purpose, but this source gives no frame-rate detail. <https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/how-puss-in-boots-the-last-wish-upped-its-animation-game> I did not find a primary source on its stepped timing. ## B5. What gives a fake away: collected statements

Each line is a specific claim with its speaker or source.

1. **Smoothness.** CG "tends to look too smooth", so the Lego team moved fast action from twos to threes or fours. <https://www.pressreader.com/australia/3d-world/20140520/282961038158328>
2. **Motion blur.** Stop-motion plates have none; its presence on puppets reads as CG or live action. Lego films banned it, Willoughbys used almost none, Missing Link compositors add it only when needed. <https://www.motionpictures.org/2014/02/how-the-lego-movie-got-its-stop-motion-look/> <https://beforesandafters.com/2020/04/22/director-kris-pearn-on-the-heightened-style-of-the-willoughbys/> <https://borisfx.com/news/oscars-2020-vfx-on-missing-link/>
3. **Non-puppet elements are the exception.** Without blur on water, splashes "looked wrong" to Animal Logic. <https://www.fxguide.com/?p=189960>
4. **Frame cloning without lighting variation.** Aardman's cloned Norbot test looked wrong because the lighting did not change. <https://beforesandafters.com/2025/01/17/a-mix-of-old-and-new/>
5. **Polish.** Selick says stop motion that is too slick has no point versus CG; he dislikes "airbrushed photorealism". <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/09/07/i-wanted-to-pull-back-from-that-sort-of-perfection-and-leave-more-mistakes-in/> <https://www.focusfeatures.com/article/henry_selick_in_conversation>
6. **A real film reading as CG.** Wildwood trailer viewers question whether it is "too polished" and note the absence of the medium's "obvious rough edges". <https://www.creativebloq.com/art/animation/laikas-final-wildwood-trailer-as-absolutely-stunning-but-would-you-know-its-stopmotion-animation> (Opinion piece; includes an uncited claim about ones.)
7. **Interpolated, extra frames.** The author of a stop-motion blog says added in-betweens make puppets seem less heavy; a commenter calls the real problem the "lack of mass". <https://www.animateclay.com/post/smooth-or-un-smooth-stop-motion-that-is-the-question> [U, blog]
8. **Too-clean, crisp surfaces.** Willoughbys production designer: "nothing should feel digital or crisp". <https://cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/how-the-willoughbys-uses-cgi-to-create-a-handmade-look-exclusive-art-189892.html>
9. **Computer-given extras.** Sam Fell: things "free from the computer" become "almost synthetic". <https://dev.animationmagazine.net/?p=3264>
10. **Simulation continuity.** A Mr Fox VFX artist: the computer "doesn't like to hold a simulation for two frames". Pinocchio's team had to work around smooth-biased sims with stepped puppets. <https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/Web-Exclusives/2010/Smart-as-a-Fox.aspx> <https://beforesandafters.com/2023/01/24/how-the-pinocchio-vfx-team-took-the-stop-motion-further/>

13. **Wrong physical scale for water.** Physically accurate droplet sizes looked wrong against a 1.5 inch figure. <https://www.fxguide.com/?p=189960>
14. **Wrong scale of the whole puppet.** Isle of Dogs dogs at half size "started to look rather puppyish". <https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/>

# Implications for a CG stop-motion pipeline (concrete, testable rules)

Confidence note: the "evidence" column cites the sources above. Numeric thresholds marked [U] are my starting hypotheses, not findings; they need a blind A/B test against real reference clips.

**R1. Cadence is authored per pose, not applied as a global frame-skip.**
Evidence: Spider-Verse animators: 2s is not "mathematically every other frame"; Selick mixes twos and threes; Lego uses twos with threes or fours for fast action and FX; Willoughbys uses twos with occasional threes, FX on threes and fours. Sources in A2, B1, B2.
Rule: bodies and props default to 2-frame holds at 24 fps (12 poses per second), threes allowed for slow moves or FX, ones allowed for fast action only when justified. Do not run a "posterize time" on the finished render.
Test: for each shot, count unique-image runs; expect a median hold of 2 with a tail of 3s, and no more than a few 1-frame runs outside fast action. Blind A/B against a metronomic 2,2,2,2 version.

**R2. Camera moves are stepped on ones, bodies stay on twos.**
Evidence: Pinocchio reshot lateral tracking moves on ones to avoid strobing at 4K. <https://beforesandafters.com/2022/12/14/how-does-cinematography-work-on-a-stop-motion-film/> Real motion control steps the camera each exposure (inference [U]).
Test: render lateral pans at several speeds; flag any shot where background displacement per held frame exceeds a strobing threshold (start at about 1 percent of frame width per 2 frames [U]) and reduce speed or add a second ease rather than switching to blur.

**R3. No motion blur on puppets, props, or the camera, with a short, explicit exception list.**
Evidence: all Lego films, Willoughbys, Spider-Verse, Missing Link plates; exceptions: Lego Batman water (partial blur), Willoughbys pans (little blur "to hide things"), fxguide's sparse Lego Movie camera blur, Missing Link compositor blur when needed, go-motion historically. Sources in B1, B2, B3.
Rule: Cycles motion blur off by default. Allowed only on a per-element flag for (a) liquids and splashes, (b) fog or smoke "shot at live speed" style layers, (c) with sign-off, a camera blur to hide a pan. Never on characters.
Test: lint the .blend or render config for use_motion_blur and any vector-blur compositing node; fail the build if present outside the exception list.

**R4. Boil is a per-material setting tied to animator touch, so it changes on new poses, but allow tiny "living hold" drift on fur and felt only.**
Evidence: Mr Fox and Isle of Dogs deliberately keep fur shimmer even on near-still puppets; Robin Robin kept felt boil "quite clean" and looser only in intimate scenes; Laika drift avoids frozen puppets; Worthington shimmers displacement. Sources in A4, A5, B2.
Rule: clay and hard-cured surfaces: boil amplitude re-seeded only on pose changes, tiny. Fur, felt, wool: allow a smaller re-seed on held frames. Keep the amplitude a per-material parameter.
Test: boil measured as frame-to-frame normal-map difference on a still crop; clay amplitude near zero on held frames and nonzero on pose changes; fur above clay.

**R5. Depth of field is computed from the real miniature, not set as "f/11 means deep".**
Evidence: real productions use small apertures (f/16 is the usual workaround per Canon's DP interview; T11 to T16 for real miniatures per Pro Video Coalition; Oliver winds the aperture to the bottom and still falls short); they use wide lenses (Pinocchio down to 15 mm, Isle of Dogs 20 mm default and 28 mm close-ups); and when depth is not enough they shoot planes separately and composite. Sources in A3, A4. <https://www.canon.co.uk/pro/stories/stop-motion-secrets/>
My calculation [U]: full-frame sensor, circle of confusion 0.03 mm. A 20 mm lens at f/11 focused at 0.7 m gives a depth of field of about 1.1 m; a 35 mm lens at f/11 at 0.4 m gives about 79 mm; a 50 mm lens at f/11 at 0.4 m gives about 37 mm. At a 1:7 miniature scale 79 mm is about 0.56 m in the story world. So "deep" in stop motion is mostly a wide-lens effect, and close-ups still fall off fast.
Rule: model a real sensor, focal length, focus distance and f-number at puppet scale; use f/11 to f/16, wide lenses (15 to 28 mm equivalent) as default; shallow focus only when motivated (macro close-up), as in Lego Batman's scale-selling tiny-lens look. Allow a two-plane focus composite (foreground and background rendered at different focus) for hero shots.
Test: depth-of-field table per shot; reject shots where the far set wall is softer than a real rig at the same lens and f-number would produce.

**R6. Fingerprints at real scale, material-specific.**
Evidence: Elliot keeps fingerprints and brush strokes; Aardman silicone is "full of fingerprints"; Lego used thumb print maps on bricks; Flushed Away skipped them as a conceit. Sources in A1, A5, B1, B2.
Rule: apply fingerprints only to materials a thumb would touch (raw clay, plasticine, unpainted silicone), at the real ridge scale of a fingertip multiplied by the miniature scale ratio; not uniform across the whole asset; painted and varnished parts get tool marks or brush strokes instead.
My arithmetic [U]: adult fingerprint ridge pitch is roughly half a millimetre. On a 1:7 puppet that reads as about 3.5 mm in story scale, which is large and obvious on a face. Check that this looks right and is not a repeated texture tile.
Test: scale bar in a macro render; a contact patch about 12 to 18 mm wide on the physical puppet [U]; no identical ridge pattern repeated on two adjacent surfaces.

**R7. Lighting variation is near zero per pose but not exactly zero, and lights are big, flat and white by default.**
Evidence: Kozachik warns against drift; Pinocchio and Missing Link cleaned flicker; but Aardman's cloned frames looked wrong with no lighting change; Isle of Dogs used flat white light and captured 16 variants per frame for effects; Pinocchio and The House use deliberate programmed flicker for fire. Sources in A3, A4, A5, B3.
Rule: stage lights constant within about 0.25 percent exposure per pose [U]; vary the Cycles noise seed per frame so each frame is a fresh sample; fire and candle lights flicker deliberately, on threes. Keep the key light large and soft.
Test: measure mean luminance of a static background patch across a held shot; target a small, nonzero standard deviation; verify that identical frames do not repeat byte-for-byte.

**R8. Do not run continuous simulations under stepped puppets. Build effects from the analogue materials stop motion really uses.**
Evidence: cotton smoke, cellophane fire, carved-soap flames, glycerin drops, cheesecloth flicker, vertical-only water, hand-built brick explosions. Sources in A1, A3, A4, B1.
Rule: bake sims at the shot cadence (twos or threes) and then art-direct; model smoke, fire, water and snow on physical stand-ins (cotton wool, cellophane, glycerin drops, rubber sheet). No volumetric smoke sims, no continuous cloth or hair sim; keyframe cloth and hair as poses.
Test: check cadence of every FX layer; confirm no cloth, hair or fluid sim cache is sampled at 24 fps.

**R9. Poses stay physically possible for the actual material and armature.**
Evidence: Lego rules (no bends, no squash and stretch, every frame buildable) <https://www.motionpictures.org/2014/02/how-the-lego-movie-got-its-stop-motion-look/>; Flushed Away replaced mouths with sculpted shapes <https://cgw.com/Publications/CGW/2006/Volume-29-Issue-10-Oct-2006-/Flushed-with-Success.aspx>; Harvie Krumpet had more than 30 mouth shapes <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvie_Krumpet> [U]; Wendell and Wild kept face seams <https://vfxvoice.com/enhancing-the-practical-mantra-of-wendell-wild/>.
Rule: no squash and stretch except where clay genuinely deforms; mouths and brows by replacement shapes with a library; allow subtle visible seams between face parts.
Test: pose lint for joint limits and volume preservation per frame.

**R10. Imperfection is built into the model and the set.**
Evidence: Elliot lumpy and asymmetrical <https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/audience-will-forgive-bad-animating-theyll-never-forgive-bad-story-adam-elliot-memoir-snail>, del Toro asymmetrical face <https://blog.frame.io/2022/12/19/guillermo-del-toro-pinocchio-animation-behind-the-scenes/>, Fell half a day on asymmetry <https://popmatters.com/an-imperfect-world-interview-with-sam-fell-and-david-bowers-directors-of-fl-2495740124.html>, Lego jitter <https://animallogic.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/The-LEGO-Movie-Bricks-Bricks-and-More-Bricks.pdf>, Theorin notches <https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2023/the-geometry-of-movement-computer-generated-imagery-in-film/from-legos-to-cgi-an-interview-with-kris-theorin/>.
Rule: no bilaterally symmetric faces or bodies; set pieces get small placement jitter and non-straight cuts; accept "90 percent fine" surface flaws (Elliot).
Test: symmetry score on each hero asset must fall below a threshold [U]; overlay mirrored mesh and require visible mismatch.

**R11. Camera is a mo-co camera, not a handheld camera, unless deliberately imitating brick films.**
Evidence: Pinocchio rotators and mo-co; Isle of Dogs strict 90 and 0 degree angles and symmetry; Lego chose handheld Steadicam as its own style. Sources in A3, A4, B1.
Rule: default smooth, stepped, mechanically plausible moves (track, pan, rotate); no handheld shake.
Test: camera path jerk limit; reject high-frequency shake.

**R12. Clean image, not heavy film grain.**
Evidence: Isle of Dogs shot at ISO 100 on a DSLR in RAW with exposures of a quarter second to two seconds; Pinocchio shot on a 5D at 4K; digital capture in stop motion is clean. Sources in A3, A4. The claim that the final look is therefore low-noise is my inference. [U]
Rule: moderate Cycles noise, denoise lightly, no stylised film grain.

**R13. Do not add atmospheric dust or haze unless it is a physical stand-in.**
Evidence: Lego Movie dropped most dust and physical smoke; Pinocchio VFX removed dust distractions. Sources in A3, B1. (Lego Movie 2's DI did add haze and flare, so this is a soft rule.) <https://animallogic.com/portfolio/projects/the-lego-movie-2/>

**R14. Validation protocol.**
Build a reference reel from the clips described above (Mr Fox, Isle of Dogs, Memoir, Pinocchio, Robin Robin) and run blind forced-choice tests with a small panel: real versus our render, then each rule switched off one at a time. Target: panel cannot beat chance on clay close-ups. [U, process suggestion]

# Conflicts with our current rules

| Our rule | What the evidence says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Bodies on twos, camera on ones | Supported by Pinocchio (twos for animation, ones for lateral camera moves) and by Lego 2 (twos default). But twos is not universal: Selick mixes twos and threes, Lego uses threes and fours for fast action, Lego 2 uses ones for a live-action-style sequence, Pinocchio's team said frame rates varied, and Laika is possibly mostly ones (uncited). | Keep, but make it pose-authored (holds of 1, 2, 3), not a flat 2. A constant 2 is a "filter" tell. |
| No motion blur ever | Mostly confirmed. Real exceptions: Lego Batman water, Willoughbys pans, fxguide's Lego Movie camera blur, Missing Link compositor blur, historic go-motion. | Replace "ever" with "never on puppets, props or camera; per-element exceptions for liquids and fog". |
| Boil changes only when the pose changes and is minimal | Minimal matches Robin Robin and, in a real sense, clay. But Fantastic Mr Fox and Isle of Dogs wanted fur to shimmer even on near-still puppets, and Laika adds breathing so puppets never freeze. | Keep for clay. Make boil a per-material parameter and allow tiny living-hold drift on fur, felt, wool. |
| Deep focus around f/11 | Matches stop-motion practice (f/16 usual, T11 to T16, wide lenses). But at puppet distances even f/11 gives only centimetres of depth with a 35 to 50 mm lens (my calculation), and professionals say matching live-action depth is "essentially impossible". Lego CG films chose shallow focus on purpose to sell scale. | Keep f/11 to f/16 but compute depth of field from real scale and lens; rely on wide lenses; allow plane-split compositing. |
| Shallow focus only when motivated, never tilt-shift gradient | Supported: fake tilt-shift only "just barely" works and fails on in-focus foreground; AI vendors list tilt-shift as the cheap miniature cue. | Keep. |
| Fingerprints at real scale | Supported by Elliot and Aardman. Opposed by Flushed Away's VFX supervisor (a "conceit"), though its co-director toyed with them. Painted puppets like Pinocchio rely on brush and tool marks instead of fingerprints. | Keep, but only on touchable raw materials; add brush or tool marks for painted parts. |
| Per-pose lighting flicker near zero | Supported by Kozachik and by VFX cleanup of flicker. But Aardman found cloned frames with no lighting change looked wrong, real stop motion has organic lens and lighting variation, and fire sources flicker deliberately. | Keep near zero for stage lights; not exactly zero; vary noise seed; flicker fire on threes. |
