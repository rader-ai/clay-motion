# LAIKA: camera, lighting, color, VFX and post (Coraline 2009 to Wildwood 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-17. Purpose: copy what translates to a CG (Blender/Cycles) stop-motion studio and list where our current rules differ.

## How to read this report

- Every factual line ends with a source link. `[U]` marks something unverified, inferred, single-source and weak, or derived by me (not stated by a source).
- When sources disagree, both figures are given.
- Quotes are short phrases only; everything else is paraphrase.
- DPs by film: Coraline, Pete Kozachik ASC ([ASC obituary](https://theasc.com/news/in-memoriam-pete-kozachik-asc-1951-2023)); ParaNorman, Tristan Oliver ([Reverie](https://www.reverie-online.com/film/tristan-oliver-interview)); Boxtrolls, John Ashlee (Prat) ([JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/ink-pixel-the-boxtrolls-270/)); Kubo, Frank Passingham ([LAIKA](https://www.laika.com/our-films/kubo)); Missing Link, Jeremy Spear [U, from memory, not confirmed by any page I read]; Wildwood, Caleb Deschanel ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildwood_(film))).
- Evidence quality: the strongest material is the Coraline AC page 1, the AC Kubo page 55 stereo excerpt, Cinefex via Graham Edwards (Boxtrolls), befores & afters / art of VFX / AWN / fxguide (Kubo, Missing Link), the Boris FX interview with LAIKA's senior paint artist, and the Canon corporate page. The weakest are fan analyses and review-site statements.
- Non-LAIKA material (Pinocchio, Isle of Dogs, Nightmare Before Christmas) is used only as a labelled comparison. It is not LAIKA practice.

## 1. Cameras, lenses, depth of field, stereo, motion control, camera moves

### 1.1 Coraline

- Capture was digital stills, frame by frame. Most units used about three dozen cooled 11-megapixel Princeton Instruments MEGAPLUS EC11000 CCD cameras ([Vision Systems](https://vision-systems.com/non-factory/article/16747537/ccd-cameras-enable-new-stop-motion-animation-methods-in-feature-film), [IMV Europe](https://www.imveurope.com/news/feature-film-coraline-takes-advantage-machine-vision)).
- AC page 1 gives 38 EC11000 (4K Kodak CCD, 36x24 mm) plus eight Nikon D80 at full speed, 46 cameras on the floor with every lens duplicated, and "more than 55 setups" running at once (the article does not reconcile 55 with 46) ([AC Feb 2009 p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)).
- Lens mount was Nikkor F; Tamron zooms (17 to 70 mm) and a 90 mm prime, Sigma lenses for close focus, and primes as short as 14 mm later in the show. Focus-ring gears were fitted by camera tech Chris Andrews ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)). The gears imply motorized focus on motion-control rigs [U inference].
- Depth of field: Kozachik says small sensors make backgrounds hard to soften, so the large 36x24 mm sensor was "a great find". No f-stops are given on page 1 ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)). This means the camera team wanted the ability to throw backgrounds soft, not only to hold deep focus.
- Vignetting from the wide lenses on a large sensor was eventually kept as "more of a production value than a defect" ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)).
- Motion control: a basic, economical rig designed with a Wisconsin machine shop; 11 new rigs joined about six existing, 17 in total. The camera application talked to Kuper motion control over two-way serial ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)).
- Eight cinematographers (Ashlee, Gentry, Stewart, Sorg, Peterson, Van't Hul, Williams, Passingham) each led a three-person unit ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)). Frank Passingham is among them, which is the lineage into Kubo.
- Stereo: each frame is shot twice by the same camera, which "travels very precisely in very small amounts" ([AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/set-coraline-where-motion-doesnt-stop)). Other World sets were built deeper than the cramped Real World sets, and Selick had to restrain the camera crew from pushing 3D further ([AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/set-coraline-where-motion-doesnt-stop), [Focus Features](https://www.focusfeatures.com/article/henry_selick_in_conversation), [BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/stop-motion-henry-selick-coraline)).
- An early stereo rain test used a horizontal slider borrowed from Kozachik, with Dragon's multi-exposure feature capturing the left and right exposures ([Dragonframe](https://www.dragonframe.com/blog/coraline-in-3d-the-dragon-connection/)).
- Frame-rate philosophy: Selick does not shoot everything "on ones" and values believable performance over perfect fluidity ([BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/stop-motion-henry-selick-coraline)).
- A fan analysis lists RED One, Nikon D80, Princeton ES1100, Nikkor/Sigma/Tamron lenses, 1.85:1 and a 3.2K DI [U: derived from database-style specs, not primary] ([Color Culture](https://colorculture.org/coraline-cinematography-analysis/)).

### 1.2 ParaNorman

- Cameras: 60 Canon EOS 5D Mark II bodies supplied by Canon (21.1 MP, DIGIC 4), USB-tethered to Dragonframe; RAW .CR2 plus JPEG per frame; footage worked at 2K with hi-res RAW kept for VFX ([SVC/Canon](https://www.svconline.com/the-wire/canon-eos-5d-mark-ii-digital-slr-cameras-streamline-production-major-new-animated-stop-motion-horror-comedy-381199), [BTL](https://www.btlnews.com/crafts/visual-fx/canon-5d-mark-ii-cameras-capture-3d-stop-motion-for-paranorman/)). fxguide and Focus Features give 63 cameras ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=35589), [Focus](https://www.focusfeatures.com/article/norman___s_numbers_and_nuggets)).
- The 5D Mark II was chosen largely for live view quality; the self-cleaning sensor meant lenses never came off to clean; none failed ([SVC/Canon](https://www.svconline.com/the-wire/canon-eos-5d-mark-ii-digital-slr-cameras-streamline-production-major-new-animated-stop-motion-horror-comedy-381199)).
- Stereo: stereo needs 24 left/right pairs per second of screen time, doubled to 48 exposures for stop-motion. Each camera sat on a computer-controlled horizontal slider that shot right then left. Side-by-side cameras were rejected because puppets were only 8 to 12 inches tall ([SVC/Canon](https://www.svconline.com/the-wire/canon-eos-5d-mark-ii-digital-slr-cameras-streamline-production-major-new-animated-stop-motion-horror-comedy-381199), [Sportsvideo](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2012/08/23/canon-digital-slr-cameras-animate-paranorman/)).
- Interaxial ranged from about 0.5 mm to 10-12 mm depending on shot ([SVC/Canon](https://www.svconline.com/the-wire/canon-eos-5d-mark-ii-digital-slr-cameras-streamline-production-major-new-animated-stop-motion-horror-comedy-381199)); the other Canon-derived write-ups give no number [single source for the numbers].
- Motion control: 53 systems (36 Kuper, 17 proprietary) plus 21 track-and-boom rigs ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=35589), [Focus](https://www.focusfeatures.com/article/norman___s_numbers_and_nuggets)).
- Camera teams: five camera teams across about 50 units; Oliver supervised all of them ([Sportsvideo](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2012/08/23/canon-digital-slr-cameras-animate-paranorman/)). Slashfilm gives 52 units at peak and says shooting ran at 24 fps in 3D ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/521162/laika-3d-color-printers-create-stopmotion-animated-movie-paranorman-50-learned-set/)).
- The 48-second walk-to-school shot needed more than 1,100 frames, several weeks of setup/animation and months of digital post ([BAFTA](https://static.bafta.org/files/paranorman-1620.pdf)). A move of that length on ones is about 1,152 unique camera poses at 24 fps [derived arithmetic].
- Oliver described the walk-to-school shot as flying a camera and lens much larger than a hand-sized puppet, with the set built so the camera's shadow fell inside house shadows ([Reverie](https://www.reverie-online.com/film/tristan-oliver-interview)).
- Directors cited Evil Dead for the camera movement ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/wordpress/features/he-hears-dead-people)).
- The studio planned Mark III bodies for the next production, with live view said to be about two stops better ([SVC/Canon](https://www.svconline.com/the-wire/canon-eos-5d-mark-ii-digital-slr-cameras-streamline-production-major-new-animated-stop-motion-horror-comedy-381199)).
- A review of the UHD disc believes capture was about 5K, DI was 2K and CG likely rendered at 2K [U: reviewer inference] ([Digital Bits](https://digitalbits.com/item/paranorman-sb-2023-uhd)).

### 1.3 The Boxtrolls

- Stop-motion was shot on Canon 5D cameras; RedShark counts 56 digital cameras, with VFX using one RED and two Sony HD cameras, and says everything was stereoscopic at 24 stereo frames per second of screen time ([RedShark](https://www.redsharknews.com/post-vfx/item/2116-stop-motion-animation-is-alive-and-well-with-the-help-of-cgi)). FotoKem confirms Canon 5D raw files ([AWN/FotoKem](https://www.awn.com/news/fotokem-provides-end-end-post-services-laikas-boxtrolls)).
- Conflict on the stereo rig: Animation Magazine says animators used side-by-side stereo camera rigs from Canon, and that complex sets were split to accommodate the stereo camera angles ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/?p=186866)). The ParaNorman-era slider approach is documented elsewhere ([SVC/Canon](https://www.svconline.com/the-wire/canon-eos-5d-mark-ii-digital-slr-cameras-streamline-production-major-new-animated-stop-motion-horror-comedy-381199)). I did not find a primary Boxtrolls rig statement [U].
- Nearly every shot used motion control, with more modern handheld-style moves than typical stop-motion ([AWN on set](https://awn.com/animationworld/on-set-laikas-boxtrolls)).
- DP John Ashlee and the camera crew built a catalog of scripted live-action-style camera moves with names like "bumpy road vibration" and "reality-show handheld" so animators could program the camera to move dynamically ([JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/ink-pixel-the-boxtrolls-270/)).
- Sets were large (1:5 scale per GeekDad); some were too tall for the ceilings and finished digitally ([GeekDad](https://geekdad.com/2014/07/boxtrolls-set-visit-2/)).

### 1.4 Kubo and the Two Strings

- Passingham shot Kubo on the Canon 5D; he stayed with DSLRs over mirrorless because the mirror protects the sensor from dust and a single shot can take three weeks ([British Cinematographer](https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/?p=87407)). The model number is not given [U: Mark III is plausible but unconfirmed].
- Stereo, from AC Sept 2016 p. 55: one camera captured both eyes for each frame on 3D sliders like Coraline's; some needed extra travel for larger interocular and all were made more compact; the fine-pitch sliders move a fraction of a millimeter; the camera sometimes moved between eye positions for as many as six exposures per frame; "tens of thousands" of eye moves overall; they sometimes animated interocular and alignment; they shot parallel and did not converge lenses, building alignment after shooting ([AC Sept 2016 p55](https://www.nxtbook.com/nxtbooks/ac/ac0916_v2/index.php?startid=55)).
- Five camera-lighting teams; 1,345 shots over 94 weeks; 80 unique sets, some full-scale, others miniatures combined with matte paintings and CG set extensions ([AWN](https://awn.com/vfxworld/explore-vfx-laika-s-kubo-and-two-strings)).
- Camera crew credits show a structured department: four lighting/camera leads, a lead camera and motion-control engineer, two motion-control operators and a technician ([LAIKA](https://www.laika.com/our-films/kubo)).
- Large moves: the helicopter shot's camera move was too big for the stage, so the tail of the shot was finished with a virtual camera ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).
- Motion control for the Garden of Eyes used a bowling ball as a trackball, with two mouse sensors driving a three-axis rig ([Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/laika-used-bowling-ball-animate-giant-eyes-kubo-two-strings-146172.html)).
- Knight says Passingham pushed the camerawork further than LAIKA had gone before ([MovieMaker](https://www.moviemaker.com/unfolding-fantasy-laikas-travis-knight-reinvorated-stop-motion-animation-became-director-kubo-two-strings)).

### 1.5 Missing Link

- Canon's own page says Missing Link was made with Canon DSLRs, naming the 5D Mark III and its successor the 5D Mark IV, and four tilt-shift lenses: TS-E 17 mm f/4L, TS-E 24 mm f/3.5L II, TS-E 45 mm f/2.8 and TS-E 90 mm f/2.8. The page does not say which lens was used where ([Canon Europe](https://www.canon-europe.com/pro/news/canon-filmed-oscar-nominees-2020/)). Tilt-shift on miniatures lets the crew place the plane of focus and correct perspective without stopping down [U: purpose inferred].
- Stereo: LAIKA's senior paint artist says they shoot stereo with one camera and a motion-control offset that resets each frame ([Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/news/oscars-2020-vfx-on-missing-link/)).
- Motion control: 47 in-house rigs, the oldest dating to 1928; the Manchuria ship set was too big to move, so camera and surrounding lighting were motorized to move around it; the elephant shot used a crane over 14 feet tall with a 12-foot reach and about 180 degrees of pan along a 35-foot path ([AWN set visit](https://awn.com/animationworld/set-visit-go-behind-scenes-laikas-missing-link), [Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507)).
- Puppets were built about 20% smaller than on earlier films to allow smaller sets; Link is 16 inches tall per AWN and 14 inches per AMC ([AWN set visit](https://awn.com/animationworld/set-visit-go-behind-scenes-laikas-missing-link), [AMC](https://www.amctheatres.com/amc-scene/behind-the-scenes-of-missing-link)). Canon states puppets were 10 to 12 inches ([Canon Europe](https://www.canon-europe.com/pro/news/canon-filmed-oscar-nominees-2020/)).
- Shot setup takes 2 to 3 days regardless of shot length ([SIGGRAPH blog](https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=9151)).
- A low camera on a 10-inch puppet made Lionel look about six inches tall in the yeti temple, fixed by a digital foreground yeti arm ([SIGGRAPH blog](https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=9151)).
- LAIKA's 2018 SIGGRAPH session says digital scenes had to merge with physical camera setups limited by how far an animator can reach a puppet, and that camera data was shared, scaled and augmented ([SIGGRAPH History](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=89083)).

### 1.6 Wildwood

- Deschanel shot outdoor tests at different light levels and f-stops to calibrate depth of field for the miniature sets ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/taking-the-longer-magical-way-home-to-wildwood/)). No f-stop values were published in what I could read.
- The film is described as using a real camera rather than a virtual one, giving authored camera language ([Discussing Film](https://discussingfilm.net/2026/10/11/wildwood-review-laika-stop-motion/)). Knight told Deschanel shooting outside was not an option ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/wildwood-first-footage-annecy-travis-knight-laika/)).
- Frame cadence: Nerds of Color reports the film is animated "on ones", a new image for every frame ([Nerds of Color](https://thenerdsofcolor.org/2026/09/17/witnessing-the-wonders-of-wildwood-at-laika-studios/)). Inverse says Knight deliberately slows the frame rate in a few sequences for a choppy classic look and does not state the cadence ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/wildwood-review-laika-animated-fantasy)). The "on ones" claim is single-source [U].
- "Jetpacks" move puppets in increments too small to see in individual frames ([Nerds of Color](https://thenerdsofcolor.org/2026/09/17/witnessing-the-wonders-of-wildwood-at-laika-studios/)). Purpose not stated [U].
- Bluescreen was built into the sets for elements added in post ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/taking-the-longer-magical-way-home-to-wildwood/)).
- Animation pace averaged 1.8 seconds per week ([Next Best Picture](https://nextbestpicture.com/a-behind-the-scenes-look-at-laikas-wildwood/)); Empire's visit counted 25 animators on the day and about 3.5 seconds per animator per week ([Empire](https://members.stg.empireonline.com/movies/features/inside-laika-studios-one-frame-at-a-time)).

### 1.7 Focus pulls

- LAIKA: only the Coraline focus-ring gears are documented ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)). No LAIKA source describes how rack focus is animated beyond that [U].
- Tooling: Dragonframe programs camera moves and lighting in one system, supports linked exposures, and exposes extra axes via DMC-32 ([Dragonframe software](http://www.dragonframe.com/dragonframe-software/)). LAIKA calls Dragon core to its commercial and feature stages ([Dragonframe blog](https://www.dragonframe.com/blog/kubo-and-the-two-strings-laika/)).
- Comparison (non-LAIKA, same DP as ParaNorman): on Isle of Dogs the crew shot some shots in separate focus planes and refocused between passes ([ASC Isle of Dogs](https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/)).

## 2. Lighting

What LAIKA sources actually say, in order of reliability. Numbers for fixture sizes and distances are mostly unpublished in readable sources.

### 2.1 Coraline

- Camera and art together built the Real World vs Other World difference; lighting restraint came from a graphic reference (Uesugi: minimal modeling but aware of light) to avoid "excess gingerbread" ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)).
- Lighting continuity was checked against neighbouring shots on stage monitors, then with a "launch frame" in the screening room, because most shots take several days and second takes are unpopular ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)).
- Dailies were projected on a D-Cinema projector (needed to judge exposure through the 3-D polarizing filters); Flipbook monitors on the floor were matched to that look, and the Avid display was too bright to judge lighting ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)).
- Fan analysis (low reliability): flat Real World light, aggressive contrast and deep blacks in the Other World [U] ([Color Culture](https://colorculture.org/coraline-cinematography-analysis/)).
- Selick says the camera department worked out a basic lighting scheme separately from the animators ([Focus Features](https://www.focusfeatures.com/article/henry_selick_in_conversation)).

### 2.2 ParaNorman

- Stage resources: 70 fluorescent fixtures and 1,102 C-stands for lighting and grip ([Focus](https://www.focusfeatures.com/article/norman___s_numbers_and_nuggets)).
- Oliver avoided green and red zombie lighting and used pale yellow and pale lilac ([Reverie](https://www.reverie-online.com/film/tristan-oliver-interview)).
- Heat was a problem: sets sometimes shifted under the lights and VFX fixed it ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/521162/laika-3d-color-printers-create-stopmotion-animated-movie-paranorman-50-learned-set/)).
- Interactive light: for the witch Aggie, separate lighting passes of the environment were shot so interactive light could be synced to her emotions; lightning and boiling clouds were tested with traditional materials then extended digitally ([BAFTA](https://static.bafta.org/files/paranorman-1620.pdf)).
- Printed faces use color-infused material that absorbs and reflects light somewhat like skin ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/wordpress/features/he-hears-dead-people), [Reverie](https://www.reverie-online.com/film/tristan-oliver-interview)).

### 2.3 The Boxtrolls

- Pinpoint LEDs were a major advance for practicals (street lamps, headlights); incandescent practicals were dropped because they run hot and can melt materials; LEDs use little power and DMX allowed programmed color and brightness ([AWN on set](https://awn.com/animationworld/on-set-laikas-boxtrolls)). The statement is about practical props, not stage lights [note scope].
- Boxtroll eyes glow from LEDs inside the heads, not post ([AWN on set](https://awn.com/animationworld/on-set-laikas-boxtrolls), [Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/?p=186866)).
- Underground/night sets used practical ambient light from lamps and furnaces; the sewer used a rotating rippled-glass plate over an up-light ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/?p=186866)).
- Sets were surveyed for stereo HDR data to light CG ([Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)).
- Ballroom: a beauty pass per frame, then a greenscreen lowered behind the puppets and lighting adjusted for a silhouette, then a matte pass; some shots added extra frames for interactive lighting, then the screen was removed and the lighting restored ([Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)).
- Set temperature drifted over weeks so motion-control plates did not always line up; paint fixed the mismatch ([Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)).

### 2.4 Kubo

- LED lanterns on the street set and "a very large light" simulating sunlight on the characters ([Stop Motion Magazine](https://stopmotionmagazine.com/kubo-strings-set-visit/)).
- Garden of Eyes: LEDs behind each pupil through counter-rotating rippled domes; a 32-inch Perspex ball filled with steel mesh caught speculars ([Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/laika-used-bowling-ball-animate-giant-eyes-kubo-two-strings-146172.html)). Lighting there used LEDs reflected in steel mesh and rotating rippled glass balls ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/546075/kubo-and-the-two-strings-travis-knight-interview)).
- Moon Beast: every pose was shot with flat white light, a shaping light, a black light for UV paint mattes and a silhouette green pass; about 100 gold Mylar balloons crumpled inside gave internal glow reflections ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=188121)).
- Dedicated lighting roles credited: supervising gaffer, four gaffers, a practical lighting engineer and a miniature lighting engineer ([LAIKA](https://www.laika.com/our-films/kubo)).
- Animators sometimes need fixes after bumping a light or stepping into a reflection; roto/paint repairs the plate ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).

### 2.5 Missing Link

- Production designer Nelson Lowry stressed lighting that lands on varied surfaces for a tactile feel; each location has a limited palette ([Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/565038/missing-link-set-visit-report/)). Per AMC each set has its own palette of three to four colors ([AMC](https://www.amctheatres.com/amc-scene/behind-the-scenes-of-missing-link)).
- Lights move with the camera on the Manchuria set ([AWN set visit](https://awn.com/animationworld/set-visit-go-behind-scenes-laikas-missing-link)). On the long train carriage set, a sliding wall moves in, lights change, the frame is captured, and the wall slides out per shot cycle ([Empire](https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/inside-laikas-stop-motion-magic)).
- Per-frame lighting passes: a beauty pass, a separately lit blue/green keying pass, and blacklight/UV passes ([Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/news/oscars-2020-vfx-on-missing-link/)).
- LAIKA avoids full green screens because they contaminate stage lighting; animators shoot a second exposure with a small green card behind the puppet for the alpha ([Intel/LAIKA transcript](https://intel.com/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/2022-08/laika-webinar-customer-spotlight-transcript.pdf)).
- Shots were frequently captured in separate passes weeks or months apart, so lighting and materials drifted between elements ([SIGGRAPH blog](https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=9151)).

### 2.6 Wildwood

- Nelson Lowry: lighting was tested extensively on how light hits surfaces; the work is "more like live action than people realize" with sets lit on stages ([Nerds of Color](https://thenerdsofcolor.org/2026/09/17/witnessing-the-wonders-of-wildwood-at-laika-studios/)).
- Atmospherics and car headlights heighten the night encounter; shadow was used to simplify busy forest backgrounds ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/taking-the-longer-magical-way-home-to-wildwood/)).
- Reviews describe "dreamy, diffused" vista light and "crepuscular glows" [U: reviewer language, not crew statements] ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/wildwood-review-laika-animated-fantasy), [Film Focus](https://www.filmfocusonline.com/post/wildwood-review)).
- Sets were built and lit as tests for the director; most assets were scanned for VFX ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/wildwood-the-exhibition-design-museum-laika-stop-motion/)).

### 2.7 Flicker and exposure stability

- ParaNorman: DSLRs overheated during multi-day shoots; sensor heat caused flicker in the blacks and most images from a hot day were unusable. Fixes: camera cooling and testing/rechecking voltage regulators. Black level was verified by sampling the first frame of a scene and comparing every later frame on a histogram, but that only worked on locked-off shots with a clear sampling area, so other methods were developed, and for some shots animators hand-keyframed the flicker out ([3DVF SIGGRAPH report](https://3dvf.com/redaction/dossier-898-compte-rendu-siggraph-2012-html/2/), [BTL News](https://www.btlnews.com/?p=31582)).
- Oliver later said Laika kept "a gang of people in VFX" fixing overnight fluctuations and flicker on ParaNorman ([Definition Magazine](https://definitionmagazine.com/features/dogged/)).
- LAIKA roto/paint also cleans up inter-frame flicker as a standard job alongside seams and rigs ([befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2020/08/26/machine-learning-rotoscoping-and-laika/)), and "de-chattering" of frame-to-frame inconsistencies on printed faces ([Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/news/oscars-2020-vfx-on-missing-link/)).
- Dragonframe's capture black-out darkens the monitor during exposure so its glow does not affect lighting ([Dragonframe software](http://www.dragonframe.com/dragonframe-software/)).
- Haze: LAIKA mostly adds it in post. Kubo's snow got a CG blizzard plus added haze, and the origami beach got volumetric fog and haze ([AWN VFX](https://awn.com/vfxworld/explore-vfx-laika-s-kubo-and-two-strings)); Boxtrolls blended CG fog with animated cheesecloth ([Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)). No LAIKA source describes physical haze on stage [U].

### 2.8 Comparison only (not LAIKA)

- Pinocchio (Passingham): all direct light tungsten, LED for bounce and indirect; key tracked about 7 to 10 feet to mimic a sunrise; a torch with small LEDs gave flicker "in every frame"; layered exposures combined in post ([Paste](https://pastemagazine.com/movies/guillermo-del-toro/guillermo-del-toros-pinocchio-frank-passingham-cinematographer-interview), [befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2022/12/14/how-does-cinematography-work-on-a-stop-motion-film/), [Awards Watch](https://awardswatch.com/?p=89671)).
- Isle of Dogs (Oliver): daylight exteriors with a flat, nearly shadowless look; LED eye lights on dimmers; 12 V tungsten bulbs for lanterns because LEDs do not warm when dimmed; the lab set captured 16 lighting iterations per frame ([ASC Isle of Dogs](https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/)). Another write-up of that film lists mostly ARRI 10K/5K/2K/300 W fresnels, Dedolights and Altman Micro Ellipses, and notes long exposures preserve depth of field ([Definition](https://definitionmagazine.com/?p=8394)).
- Nightmare Before Christmas (Kozachik, 1993, film): mostly 17 to 24 mm lenses; wider stops used to separate subjects; lights dimmed up and down, never switched on full; power conditioners held mains within one volt of 117 V ([ASC](https://theasc.com/articles/nightmare-before-christmas)).

## 3. Color pipeline

- Coraline: dailies on a D-Cinema projector with floor monitors matched to it (above) ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)). A fan analysis names colorist Tim Peeler and a 2383/3383 print look [U] ([Color Culture](https://colorculture.org/coraline-cinematography-analysis/)).
- Boxtrolls: FotoKem ran an end-to-end EXR workflow in 16-bit float; Canon 5D raw was converted to EXR; the colorist (John Daro) graded in Mistika in linear gamma 1.0 using an invertible curve, and built a custom node with one correction for the base range and one for extended highlights. A library of stills and color settings carried from trailer to DI ([AWN/FotoKem](https://www.awn.com/news/fotokem-provides-end-end-post-services-laikas-boxtrolls)).
- Kubo: Technicolor handled the 2D, 3D and HDR color grades ([AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/awn-exclusive-travis-knight-s-unique-approach-stop-motion-storytelling)). A user-maintained database lists 2.39:1 and DI colorist Mike Sowa [U] ([ShotOnWhat](https://shotonwhat.com/kubo-and-the-two-strings-2016)).
- VFX supply articulate mattes for color grading ([Art of VFX Kubo](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).
- Kubo and ParaNorman UHD releases were reported as 2K DIs, regraded for HDR10 and Dolby Vision under LAIKA supervision [U: reviewer statements] ([Digital Bits Kubo](https://digitalbits.com/item/kubo-two-strings-shout-2023-uhd), [Digital Bits ParaNorman](https://digitalbits.com/item/paranorman-sb-2023-uhd)).
- Missing Link: final color grade in February 2019 per the VFX lead interview ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507)). Palette is art-led: bold 1940s/50s National Geographic color, three to four colors per set, a color script following the action ([AMC](https://www.amctheatres.com/amc-scene/behind-the-scenes-of-missing-link), [Box Office Pro](https://www.boxofficepro.com/no-stopping-laika-a-tour-of-the-pioneering-stop-motion-animation-studio-behind-missing-link/), [Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/565038/missing-link-set-visit-report/)).
- Costume color was tuned to survive the camera (houndstooth built from interlocking stars to avoid moire; a father's jacket on Coraline was re-dyed to stop moire) ([Box Office Pro](https://www.boxofficepro.com/no-stopping-laika-a-tour-of-the-pioneering-stop-motion-animation-studio-behind-missing-link/)).
- Kubo's Moon Beast palette: warm purple and gold in shadows and midtones from the flat pass, selective green/blue and gold shimmer from the shaping pass ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=188121)).
- Consistency across hundreds of sets: documented mechanisms are projected dailies and matched monitors (Coraline), shared EXR/linear pipeline (Boxtrolls), Dragonframe's exposure tools (clipping view, digital densitometer, color detection) ([Dragonframe software](http://www.dragonframe.com/dragonframe-software/)). A LUT-everywhere workflow is documented for Isle of Dogs (not LAIKA) ([ASC Isle of Dogs](https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/)). I found no LAIKA source describing a single show LUT [U].
- Film palettes: Coraline's Real World drab vs Other World distorted mirror with an Amelie reference ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)); Kubo drew on woodblock artist Kiyoshi Saito ([TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/kubo-two-strings-biggest-stop-motion-animation), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubo_and_the_Two_Strings)); Wildwood's Prue wears a navy peacoat and orange scarf, the Coyote Army red ([Nerds of Color](https://thenerdsofcolor.org/2026/09/17/witnessing-the-wonders-of-wildwood-at-laika-studios/)).

## 4. VFX and post

### 4.1 Cleanup: seams, rigs, faces

- Seam removal by roto and paint began on Coraline in 2009 ([befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2020/08/26/machine-learning-rotoscoping-and-laika/)). Selick says the seams were left visible on purpose in design, then painted out because the backer found them distracting ([BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/stop-motion-henry-selick-coraline)).
- Coraline VFX could also create in-between frames or smooth problem shots when reshooting was too costly (head of VFX Brian Van't Hul) ([AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/set-coraline-where-motion-doesnt-stop)). That is frame interpolation used as a repair tool.
- ParaNorman: greenscreen replacement, rig removal, facial cast-line removal and lighting matching; about 60 VFX staff; an estimated 35 to 40 percent of shots show VFX ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/wordpress/features/he-hears-dead-people)).
- Boxtrolls: most faces were two-part, so every affected frame needed seam removal; flying characters held by armatures were erased in post ([Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/), [GeekDad](https://geekdad.com/2014/07/boxtrolls-set-visit-2/)).
- Kubo: roto/paint handled puppet cosmetics, rig removal and plate repair, blended the gap between face and eyelid, and replaced Kubo's hair with CG in a single shot ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167), [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/feature-film/laika-used-bowling-ball-animate-giant-eyes-kubo-two-strings-146172.html)). The giant skeleton's support cables were painted out and surfaces rebuilt ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).
- Missing Link: over 1,000 shots needed rig removal; 106,000+ printed faces received cosmetic work; 446 shots were 2D/seam cleanup only; remote "backpacks" were painted out ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507), [befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2019/04/12/how-stop-motion-and-vfx-meet-in-laikas-missing-link)).
- Machine learning (Intel) now generates roto shapes for the face split line inside Nuke from a few tracking points; the training set was only five or six shots; roto was bid at about 50 frames per day ([befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2020/08/26/machine-learning-rotoscoping-and-laika/), [Intel/LAIKA transcript](https://intel.com/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/2022-08/laika-webinar-customer-spotlight-transcript.pdf)).

### 4.2 Motion blur (important for our rule)

- Missing Link, LAIKA's senior paint artist: the "lack of motion blur in our filmed plates" is the most obvious difference from live action; when a shot needs blur, it is added in post by the compositing team ([Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/news/oscars-2020-vfx-on-missing-link/)).
- Kubo fights were done with practical puppets, no CG duplicates; animators used in-camera blur, pieces of clear plastic or multiple arms to reduce strobing; any remaining strobing was smoothed with extra in-camera effort or spot motion blur in post ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).
- ParaNorman: puppet makers printed Norman faces with three noses to get "practical motion blur", and multiple noses/eyes simulated blur for action; a toothpaste sequence used 150 faces ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/wordpress/features/he-hears-dead-people), [Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/521162/laika-3d-color-printers-create-stopmotion-animated-movie-paranorman-50-learned-set/)).
- Selick's stance: imperfections and stepped motion are part of the medium; he does not shoot everything on ones ([BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/stop-motion-henry-selick-coraline)).
- Motion blur on CG water, crowds and other CG elements: not stated in any source I could read [U]. Kubo's water sources discuss matching puppet tracks and motion but not blur settings ([fxguide water](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=187493)).
- Grain or noise added in post: no LAIKA source states this [U]. Sensor noise is inherent to the 5D capture; Canon noted the Mark III has about two stops less noise than the Mark II ([SVC/Canon](https://www.svconline.com/the-wire/canon-eos-5d-mark-ii-digital-slr-cameras-streamline-production-major-new-animated-stop-motion-horror-comedy-381199)).

### 4.3 Timing of CG relative to puppets (ones, twos)

- Boxtrolls: faces are often held two or three frames before swapping, and the CG team wrote scripts to reproduce that granular texture on CG puppets ([Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)).
- CG characters report to the same animation director as the stage animators (Boxtrolls) and to the same Animation Supervisor on Missing Link (Brad Schiff), and facial rigs were checked against puppet face approvals ([Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/), [Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507)).
- Emerson: a computer "will always want to give you something that's perfect unless you tell it not to" ([SIGGRAPH blog](https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=9151)).
- Kubo water: LAIKA said the stop-motion was smooth enough that an accurate object track for the puppets was the main requirement, and that integration relied mostly on motion ([fxguide water](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=187493)). Whether sims were stepped to a stop-motion cadence is not stated [U].
- Render scheduling note: Kubo leads decided nightly which water elements to render "on 10's" at 1k or 2k (fog is fine at 1k) ([fxguide water](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=187493)). That reads as a review cadence, not an animation cadence [U].

### 4.4 CG elements and how they were made to match

- Boxtrolls: hero puppets are always physical; first two crowd rows physical, deeper ones CG; 20 to 30 buildings rendered at character level; hair modeled as curve patches interpolated into ribbons of about 200 hairs; costumes with procedural cloth in Slim; target was "stylised photoreal", matching miniature materials not real ones ([Post Magazine](https://www.postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2014/October-1-2014/Animation-The-Boxtrolls.aspx), [Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)). RedShark counts about 185 shots with CG characters and a ballroom with 150 digital extras ([RedShark](https://www.redsharknews.com/post-vfx/item/2116-stop-motion-animation-is-alive-and-well-with-the-help-of-cgi)). Empire counts 81 CG Boxtrolls and 96 CG humans [U: different counting basis] ([Empire](https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/inside-laikas-stop-motion-magic)).
- ParaNorman: crowds with the largest shot over 4,000 CG extras; set extensions from scanned real sets; Aggie's dress, hair and energy as digital elements matchmoved to the puppet; ghosts were practical puppets enhanced with 2D/3D tracking ([BAFTA](https://static.bafta.org/files/paranorman-1620.pdf)).
- Kubo ocean: practical water tests (garbage bags over iron grids, shower curtains) led to a CG ocean. About eight months to build the water system and roughly a year more to settle a workflow; Houdini sims with Mari-painted wave textures; a stylized look based on Japanese woodblock prints, with wood-grain textures blended with procedural noise and fractals; paper churn references made by the art department ([AWN ocean](https://awn.com/vfxworld/stop-motion-and-vfx-converge-vast-oceans-kubo-and-two-strings), [fxguide water](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=187493), [Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).
- Kubo plates: seams and rigs removed, plates repositioned into raw digital environments; matte-painted night sky; volumetric clouds; foam/churn/spray fluid sims; wave-parting effect in the composite; CG crowds blocked as gray shapes then lit with the set lighting and given shadows matched to the plate ([AWN VFX](https://awn.com/vfxworld/explore-vfx-laika-s-kubo-and-two-strings)).
- Kubo Moon Beast: LAIKA's first fully 3D-printed puppet; per-pose passes of flat light, shaping light, black light (UV) and green silhouette; invisible UV paint isolated regions as mattes in color channels; the transformation from the grandfather was a hybrid of an animated husk and a separate puppet plate, with a CG morph rejected as too synthetic ([fxguide Moon Beast](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=188121), [Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).
- Kubo giant skeleton: 16 to 18 feet tall (sources differ: 16 ft per Wikipedia/Stop Motion Magazine, 18 ft per Art of VFX, 22 ft wingspan), mounted on a hexapod from the waist up; a three-foot version handled many shots ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubo_and_the_Two_Strings), [TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/kubo-two-strings-biggest-stop-motion-animation), [Slashfilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/546075/kubo-and-the-two-strings-travis-knight-interview)).
- Kubo fire was done in Maya; snow and most FX in Houdini; skies built in VUE/Photoshop and composited in Nuke; Garden of Eyes enhanced in Nuke with a 2.5D approach (atmospheric perspective, plankton, volumetrics) ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).
- Origami birds and mosquitoes were photoreal CG recreations of practical puppets, with custom procedural wing tools ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)).
- Missing Link water and ice: Houdini FLIP with an unbounded domain, a guided "narrow band" ocean layer for a rogue wave, instanced hero waves and dual rest fields refreshed about every 50 frames; a practical ice bridge of 64 resin blocks vs a CG one with 37,000 parts that had to match "pretty tight"; underwater shots with 2.5D particulates in Nuke ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507), [befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2019/04/12/how-stop-motion-and-vfx-meet-in-laikas-missing-link)).
- Missing Link CG characters: turntables of reference puppets were shot under three light sources, each light individually, plus HDRIs for matching; CG was reviewed side by side with the real puppet; the saloon bar was rebuilt digitally so CG characters could cast shadows ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507), [SIGGRAPH blog](https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=9151)).
- Missing Link fur: scale tips painted with UV paint; one channel drove a distortion alpha in Nuke ([befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2019/04/12/how-stop-motion-and-vfx-meet-in-laikas-missing-link)).
- Wildwood: foliage motion used UV-reactive paint to isolate leaves and add shimmer; vapor wisps around flying puppets; digital characters coordinated with facial animation supervision; digital environment extension of Forest Park ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/taking-the-longer-magical-way-home-to-wildwood/)).
- Fire, smoke, water at LAIKA before CG: replacement animation with 3D-printed frames (Coraline), gelatins (ParaNorman) ([fxguide water via SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/news/fxguide-kubo-and-the-two-strings-water-effects/)); cotton and cheesecloth for storms and skies ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/wordpress/features/he-hears-dead-people), [Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)).

### 4.5 Pipeline and renderer

- Tools named for Missing Link: Maya, Houdini, Katana for lighting, RenderMan (RIS), Nuke, Silhouette (cosmetic/paint), Substance, Shotgun ([SIGGRAPH blog](https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=9151), [Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507)). RIS improved lighting stability and enabled low-sample desk-side lighting dailies, adopted late in Kubo ([SIGGRAPH blog](https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=9151)).
- Lighters worked while stage animation continued and could call the DP when HDRIs or survey data were not enough ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507)).
- Renderer is a path tracer (RIS) on a CPU farm of Intel Xeon machines ([Intel/LAIKA transcript](https://intel.com/content/dam/www/central-libraries/us/en/documents/2022-08/laika-webinar-customer-spotlight-transcript.pdf)).

## Implications for a CG stop-motion pipeline

Rules below are proposals. Items marked [derived] are my arithmetic or reasoning from the sources, not a LAIKA statement. Each has a test.

### Camera and lens

1. Camera preset: 36x24 mm sensor, spherical lenses between 14 and 90 mm; wide prime (14 to 28 mm) as the default for "massive" sets and a tilt-shift-style preset (17, 24, 45, 90 mm with shift) for architectural lines. Evidence: Coraline 14 to 90 mm, Missing Link TS-E 17/24/45/90. Test: lens list is validated against that set; vertical lines stay parallel when `shift_y` is used (vertical convergence under 0.1 degrees) [derived test].
2. Tilt is not native in Blender cameras. Emulate Scheimpflug by driving a defocus pass from the distance to a tilted focal plane in the compositor, or use a custom lens. Test: the ground plane of a tabletop set stays sharp from foreground to background at the tilted focus while the puppet's far side softens [derived].
3. Define aperture by full-scale equivalent. For a scene at scale s, keeping the same field of view and look as a full-size scene needs the stop to scale by 1/s: N_miniature = N_full / s [derived]. At 1:6 (10 to 12 inch puppets), f/11 to f/16 equals full-scale f/1.8 to f/2.8. State both numbers on every shot. Test: a unit test that converts and asserts f/16 at 1:6 reproduces the circle of confusion of f/2.7 at 1:1 within 5%.
4. Keep shallow focus available as a normal tool, not an exception: Coraline's DP valued the big sensor for softening backgrounds, and Deschanel calibrated DOF with f-stop tests per set. Test: every shot has a recorded "equivalent full-scale stop" and a reason code.
5. Camera moves: sample the camera on every frame (ones). A 48-second continuous move (ParaNorman) is about 1,152 distinct poses [derived]. Test: the exported camera F-curve has a key on every frame and no sub-frame evaluation.
6. Build a library of named scripted moves ("bumpy road vibration", "reality-show handheld" from Boxtrolls) as per-frame offset recipes. Test: library entries are deterministic given a seed and applied additively to the base path.
7. Stage envelope validator: flag camera poses outside the set bounding box or beyond animator reach, and require an explicit "virtual camera" tag for any segment that leaves the physical stage (Kubo helicopter tail, Missing Link reach limit). Test: validator output on a sample path.
8. Focus pulls: key focus distance on every frame as a motion-control axis (Coraline gears; Dragonframe axes). Test: focus distance has a key on every frame and eases on the same frames as the camera move.
9. If stereo is ever needed: parallel cameras with off-axis shift, interaxial between 0.5 and 12 mm for 10 to 12 inch puppets, animated I/O allowed, up to six sub-exposures per frame (Kubo). Test: render left/right with parallel cameras and verify no vertical disparity.

### Frame cadence

10. Separate cadence channels: camera on ones; body animation on twos by default only where there is no lateral camera motion across the puppet; faces held two or three frames (Boxtrolls); CG crowds and extras animated with the same cadence as nearby puppets. Test: per-asset cadence report; extras share their nearest puppet's phase.
11. Strobing guard (non-LAIKA source: Pinocchio moved animation from twos to ones for lateral moves in 4K; Wildwood is reported on ones). Compute screen-space puppet displacement per frame; if over a threshold, force ones or enable practical blur. Threshold is not published [U]; calibrate by test renders.

### Motion blur

12. Default off for puppets and camera (plates have none). Allow an artist-directed "practical blur" tool: (a) multi-image smear geometry (the CG analog of the triple-nose faces and clear plastic), (b) a limited compositor vector blur applied to named elements, as LAIKA does in Missing Link and Kubo post. Test: a flag per shot that must be set to enable it; blurred pixels limited to listed objects; compare strobing metric with and without.
13. CG water, fire and smoke: sample at the cadence of the surrounding plate and treat blur the same way as puppets; if a sim must run continuous, record that as an exception. LAIKA's practice is not published [U].

### Lighting

14. Light the CG like the stage: real fixtures at real sizes and distances, LED-style practicals with small emitters, a large far source for sun (Kubo) and diffused bounce for soft daylight vistas (Wildwood reviews). Test: shadow penumbra width scales with source size over distance, checked on a calibration card.
15. Animate light per frame with key lights as motion-control axes (Dragonframe keyframes, Missing Link motorized lights, Pinocchio sunrise as a non-LAIKA example). Test: light transforms keyed on every frame for shots with moving light.
16. Output light groups every render: key, fill, rim, practicals, emissive, plus ID/Cryptomatte. This is the CG analog of LAIKA's per-frame passes (flat, shaping, UV, green matte). Test: sum of light groups equals the beauty pass within 1e-3 (linear).
17. No green screen contamination: use ID mattes, never chroma, since LAIKA avoids green because it contaminates lighting. Test: no green emissive geometry in beauty.
18. Atmosphere in comp: depth-based haze and 2.5D atmospheric perspective (Kubo Garden of Eyes, snow haze) instead of volumetric scatter. Test: Z-depth haze with a single density control per sequence.

### Flicker and imperfection

19. Capture-side exposure variation is zero; deliberate variation is per-object, seeded and logged. LAIKA fought capture flicker with cooling, regulators and VFX fixes (ParaNorman) and still repairs flicker and face chatter in roto/paint. Test: frame-to-frame mean luminance of a static region has standard deviation under a set threshold [threshold derived from tests].
20. Add a small, named imperfection layer rather than random noise: held face frames, UV-paint-style fur wobble, mild vignette (Coraline kept it as a production value). Test: layer is on/off per show and logged.

### Color

21. Store scene-linear 16-bit half EXR; apply one show transform (LUT) downstream; do trims per sequence and per deliverable (SDR/HDR/3D), as Boxtrolls (linear EXR in Mistika) and Kubo (Technicolor 2D/3D/HDR) did. Test: no display transform baked into stored renders; an automated round-trip EXR to LUT to EXR check.
22. Review everything through one calibrated chain; Coraline matched floor monitors to the projector and rejected the editor's brighter display. Test: one OCIO config used by all review tools.
23. Set-level palettes: limit each set to three or four dominant hues (Missing Link) and follow a color script per sequence. Test: palette extraction per set flags more than four dominant hue clusters [derived threshold].

### VFX and validation

24. Validate CG against photographed reference: turntable of the real material under three separate lights plus HDRIs (Missing Link look development). Test: side-by-side difference metric between render and reference under each light.
25. Sky policy: sky cards/cycs as geometry or comp plates (cheesecloth sky rigs, matte-painted skies in Kubo/Boxtrolls), not as world lighting; if HDRIs are used, they are stage captures used to match inserted CG, not invented illumination. Test: world strength is zero; any HDRI is tagged "stage capture".
26. Frame interpolation only as a repair tool (Coraline VFX could build in-betweens for problem shots). Test: interpolated frames tagged in metadata and capped per shot.

## Conflicts with our current rules

Our rules were checked against the files in `/home/claude/rnd/look` (true-metre scene, 35 mm lens, f/11, 36 mm sensor, near-black world, post noise and vignette) only for context. Each verdict is about the sources, not our code.

| Our rule | Verdict | What LAIKA sources say |
|---|---|---|
| No motion blur ever | **Contradicted** | Plates have no native blur, but blur is added in post when needed (Missing Link, [Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/news/oscars-2020-vfx-on-missing-link/)); Kubo fights used in-camera blur aids and "spot motion blur in post" ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)); ParaNorman printed triple-nose faces as "practical motion blur" ([Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/wordpress/features/he-hears-dead-people)). Recommendation: default off, per-shot opt-in. |
| Deep focus around f/11 to f/16 equivalent; shallow only when motivated | **Partly supported, incomplete** | No LAIKA f-stop is published in anything I could read. LAIKA's DP wanted the big sensor to soften backgrounds ([AC p1](https://www.theasc.com/ac_magazine/February2009/Coraline/page1.php)); Missing Link used tilt-shift lenses ([Canon](https://www.canon-europe.com/pro/news/canon-filmed-oscar-nominees-2020/)); Wildwood calibrated DOF by f-stop tests ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/taking-the-longer-magical-way-home-to-wildwood/)). Our "f/11 to f/16" matches full-scale f/1.8 to f/2.8 at 1:6 only if stated as a scale equivalent [derived]. Not contradicted, but "deep by default" is not documented as LAIKA practice [U]. |
| Small hard key lights at true distances | **Not supported; partly at odds** | LAIKA sources describe a very large light for sun in Kubo ([Stop Motion Magazine](https://stopmotionmagazine.com/kubo-strings-set-visit/)), LED small emitters for practicals, and "dreamy, diffused" vista light in Wildwood reviews [U] ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/wildwood-review-laika-animated-fantasy)). The same DP as ParaNorman used one large diffused source for daylight on Isle of Dogs (not LAIKA) ([ASC](https://theasc.com/article/animal-kingdom-isle-of-dogs/)). "True distances" itself is consistent with how a stage works. "Small and hard" as a global rule is not documented. |
| No sky or HDRI lighting | **Partly contradicted** | LAIKA CG lighting uses HDRIs and survey data captured on the stage ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=25507), [Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)), and skies are CG or matte painted (Kubo, Boxtrolls) ([Art of VFX](https://www.artofvfx.com/?p=16167)). Both are matching tools, not the lighting of puppets that exist on a stage. Our rule holds if scoped to "no invented environment lighting". |
| Flicker near zero, never per frame | **Mostly supported, with exceptions** | LAIKA targets near-zero capture flicker (cooling, regulators, histogram checks) but still needed VFX staff to fix it on ParaNorman ([3DVF](https://3dvf.com/redaction/dossier-898-compte-rendu-siggraph-2012-html/2/), [Definition](https://definitionmagazine.com/features/dogged/)). Deliberate per-frame light changes are normal: interactive passes for Aggie ([BAFTA](https://static.bafta.org/files/paranorman-1620.pdf)), motorized lights on Missing Link ([AWN](https://awn.com/animationworld/set-visit-go-behind-scenes-laikas-missing-link)). Allow deliberate per-object variation; keep global exposure variance zero. |
| One show LUT per production | **Not contradicted; evidence thin** | Boxtrolls used a single linear EXR chain into a Mistika grade ([FotoKem](https://www.awn.com/news/fotokem-provides-end-end-post-services-laikas-boxtrolls)); Kubo had separate 2D, 3D and HDR grades ([AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/awn-exclusive-travis-knight-s-unique-approach-stop-motion-storytelling)). Grading is shot- and sequence-level in the DI; a LAIKA source describing a single show LUT was not found [U]. Treat "one LUT" as the base transform, with trims allowed. |
| Camera on ones, bodies on twos | **Camera on ones supported; bodies on twos not supported as a global rule** | Moves are programmed per frame on motion control. Coraline mixes cadences ([BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/stop-motion-henry-selick-coraline)); Wildwood is reported on ones with a few deliberately slower sections [single source, U] ([Nerds of Color](https://thenerdsofcolor.org/2026/09/17/witnessing-the-wonders-of-wildwood-at-laika-studios/), [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/wildwood-review-laika-animated-fantasy)); Boxtrolls faces held two or three frames ([Cinefex via Graham Edwards](https://graham-edwards.com/2021/04/09/the-hybrid-world-of-the-boxtrolls/)). Pinocchio moved from twos to ones for lateral moves (not LAIKA) ([befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2022/12/14/how-does-cinematography-work-on-a-stop-motion-film/)). |

Additional differences worth knowing:

- Post vignette and sensor noise: consistent with LAIKA (vignette kept as a production value on Coraline; 5D noise is inherent). No LAIKA source says grain is added in post [U].
- Frame interpolation: LAIKA VFX used it for problem shots on Coraline ([AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/set-coraline-where-motion-doesnt-stop)). We have no rule on it.
- LAIKA works in a compositing-first model (multi-pass plates, green mattes, UV mattes, CG extras), so look changes are made in comp. A pure single-beauty render pipeline differs.
