# How clay and stop-motion studios staff a production (research, Oct 17, 2026)

Evidence: ~50 searches/fetches. Strongest: Aardman job postings and role profiles, IMDb credit aggregates (approximate), trade features. [U] = unverified. Formal sign-off by seniority level is not documented anywhere; level descriptions come from postings and credits. "Pop-through" not found by this researcher (the pipeline researcher found it in Nightmare Before Christmas sources and UK NOS). Nearest Aardman practice: animator rehearses on live-action video (LAV), editorial cuts a rough "move-through" blocking pass, then finals.

## 0. Scale anchors
- Vengeance Most Fowl (Aardman): 200+ crew; animators grew 2 to 32-35; ~40 stages at peak; 4.2-5 s/animator/week (~2 min/week total); ~5 years.
- Chicken Run: Dawn of the Nugget: ~350 crew; 30 animators; 45 units at peak; 119 sets; 130 animatable props.
- Pinocchio: 41 animators on 60 sets at peak; 3.8 s/week; puppets built in Manchester (Mackinnon & Saunders), Portland, Guadalajara.
- Missing Link (Laika): 31 animators, 97 units, 92 weeks; 4.3 s/week target (~2 for action); ~450 on-set crew at peak.
- Wildwood (Laika, US release Oct 23, 2026): 136 sets, 231 puppets, ~140,000 printed faces, 60-70 in rapid prototyping.
- Memoir of a Snail: 7 animators; ~33 weeks shooting after 16 weeks prep; ~200 sets.
- Short form: Aardman Frubes ad, 6 people, 4 weeks. One 6th Studio ad, 15 credits. Thatchers (Aardman), 40 people, 480+ animation hours for 40 s.

## Seniority pattern (from postings)
Head/Supervisor (10+ yrs; owns department plan, works across departments) > Lead/Team Leader (5+ yrs; sets tasks and deadlines, mentors) > Senior (hardest work) > mid (2-3 yrs; owns assigned shots or builds unsupervised) > Junior/Assistant (~1 yr; prep, maintenance, repetitive work) > trainee/runner. Years cited: Animation Supervisor 10+, Supervising Rigger 7+, Puppet Team Leader 5+, Rigger 3, Animator 2+. Review-by-level is [U].
Laika titles: Head of Production, Head of Animation, Director of Rapid Prototyping, Puppet Fabrication Creative Supervisor, Production Designer, Head of Costume, Head of Character and Practical FX.

## 1. Production line
- Executive Producer: development, creative oversight, financier relationship; not daily running. Takes notes from board/financier; gives notes to director and producer. Feature 1-5; commercial 0-1 (agency and client act as EP layer).
- Producer: schedule, budget, hiring, department heads; balances client, director vision, budget. Laika's producer re-sequences shots when puppets finish late and asks whether a shot can go with the parts that are ready. Feature 1-3; short 1.
- Line Producer > Production Manager > Floor PM > Coordinator > runner. Line Producer owns script breakdown, daily schedule, finances. Laika's PM tracks animator output vs target. Good: units never starve for puppets. Failure: units outrun puppet readiness (Missing Link built duplicates, shot partial scenes on green).

## 2. Direction and story
- Director / Co-director: vision, performance, shot approval; directors approve or reject each shot, rejected shots reshot (VMF weekly review of prior week). Del Toro kept one animator per sequence for consistency. VMF animators act out shots on video but follow intent, not copy. Co-director patterns: Pinocchio (del Toro vision; Gustafson, an animator, emotion and story); VMF (Park and Crossingham, Becher second unit). Commercial: 1 director who also animates and troubleshoots.
- Writer: script and story logic. Writer-directors rewrite against boards; Adam Elliot did 16 drafts and ~1,500 hand-drawn panels.
- Head of Story > story artists > revisionists. Pinocchio ~10 story credits; story reels took ~12 months, cut by a dedicated editor with the co-director. Failure: boarded camera moves the physical set can't support.
- Voice: dialogue recorded first; animators work to it. Casting/session ownership undocumented [U].

## 3. Design
- Production Designer: world, palette, physical-vs-digital tiering, sign-off on puppet surface treatments. Missing Link's PD tiered hard shots by cost with DP and VFX supervisor; used previs to avoid overbuilding. PD > Art Director > Assistant AD > set designers.
- Art Director / Set Designer / Set Builder / Props: drawings and shop delivery. Memoir ~5,000-7,000 props. Short: fabricators double as set builders.
- Visual Development / Character Designer / Colour Script: Missing Link four concept artists; Pinocchio ~4 concept + 3 character designers. Colour script from PD team or DP. Good: designs buildable at puppet scale.

## 4. Fabrication
- Head of Puppets: every puppet from drawing to camera; fidelity, animator usability, consistency across copies. Look notes from PD, performance notes from animation; builds reviewed with director. On Pinocchio, puppet fabrication supervisor Georgina Hayns ran daily cross-studio calls across the build sites; ShadowMachine's puppet unit alone was 50-60 people. Ladder: Head of Puppets > Puppet Team Leader > senior > puppet maker > junior (maintains puppets back from set, repetitive casting). Hero puppet 3-12 months. Feature 30-100 people.
- Sculptor (maquette, director approves before casting at Laika), mould makers, armature makers (100-250 part skeletons), costume makers (weights, hidden rig openings). Good: survives a year+ of handling. Failure: stiff armature changes performance; fragile hands.
- Replacement faces / Rapid Prototyping (Laika): digital animators build expression libraries; shot-specific faces go to the director as playblasts, then print and finish. ~2 hours per face; 24 faces per second of film. Aardman: clay heads and mouth sets (14 per puppet, 24 for Molly).

## 5. Animation
- Animation Supervisor / Director: performance quality, shot allocation, puppet and rig requirements; mentors team. VMF ladder: Supervising Animator > 2 Lead > 4 Senior > ~6+ Animators > Senior Assistant > Assistant. Pinocchio: 7 lead, ~13 senior, ~30 animators, 2 facial animation leads.
- Lead/Senior/Animator: perform shots to the brief, take director notes, keep puppets on model. Seniors take the hardest shots (Gromit shots hardest). Rate 3.8-5 s/week features, ~2 for action. Good: ~1% reshoot rate (VMF, helped by live-action reference). Failure: a missed shot cost a week to reshoot (Shaun).
- Assistant Animator: cleans and tests puppet parts, keeps animators supplied, animates as needed. UK junior standard: rehearsal film, agree key frames with the director.
- Rigging: Supervising Rigger > Lead > Rigger. Rigs built to ease VFX removal. Laika rigged ~2 shots/day.

## 6. Camera and VFX
- DP: look, lighting plans, lens and kit. Pinocchio: four lighting/camera teams of ~10-12 sets each. VMF: 2 senior lighting cameras, 2 gaffers, motion control engineer.
- VFX Supervisor links director and VFX, manages compositors; on-set VFX supervisor ensures elements are captured; paint/roto artists clean up and de-rig. Missing Link 1,486 VFX shots; Memoir ~600.

## 7. Post and sound
- Editor > Associate > 1st AE > 2nd AE. Pinocchio: two editors (story reels; animation assemblies and VFX maps), 3-5 shot mini-cuts to the director. Failure: temp music changes force constant re-fitting.
- Script Supervisor: none found in stop motion; continuity carried by animators, coordinators, editorial [U].
- Sound: VMF dialogue editor, 2 SFX editors, 1 Foley artist, 2 mixers, 2 composers, music editor.

## 8. Technical
Aardman CTO owns technology teams. Laika Technology group (IT, TD, software); RP team wrote custom printing software.

## Answers
1. Creative line (answers to Director): story, design, fabrication, animation, camera, VFX supervision, editorial, music, sound. Production line (answers to Producer): line producer, PM, coordinators, ADs, post supervisor, VFX producer. Director says no on performance, design, shot acceptance; Producer says no on schedule, budget, headcount, scope [inferred].
2. Sign-off: shot = directors; puppet = PD on surface, animation on function, director on maquette; set = PD with DP and VFX sup [U]; edit = director with editor. Veto points: network execs pre-production (Robot Chicken), legal script review; commercials add director, agency, client approvals at script, boards, animatic.
3. Compression: Frubes 6 people. One 6th: one person was producer, writer-director, DP, editor, set designer and builder. Memoir: Elliot wrote, directed, designed; PM doubled as VFX supervisor. Robot Chicken: ~100 puppets/episode, ~6 weeks fabrication, ~11 minutes shot/week, 225 staff, 40 stages (2018).

## Researcher's agent-mapping advice
Mirror the Director/Producer split first; one weekly shot-review gate with reshoot rate as the metric; a fabrication supervisor owning consistency across copies; a joint rigging-VFX "plate-ready" check.
