# Creative direction from the Executive Producer

This file holds Chris's creative direction for the studio. The Creative Director turns it into house rules in `studio/STYLE_BIBLE.md`; every Production Designer turns it into a material bible for their production.

## Direction, Oct 17, 2026

> Real textures and fibers and earthy components. But they need to have a connection and a thread between them all creatively. It needs to feel intentional.

## What that means in practice (draft, pending Chris's choices below)

- **Real.** Textures come from real materials wherever possible: an owned library captured by photographing actual clay, wool, felt, linen, jute, wood, cork and stone, filled in with CC0 scans. Procedural texture is used only where it holds up next to the real captures.
- **Fibers.** Wool roving and felt, yarn, linen, jute, twine, embroidery thread. They read as real through fuzz, stray fibers, pilling, frayed edges and visible stitches, at the right scale for a 15 to 25 cm puppet.
- **Earthy.** Clay and terracotta, raw and carved wood, cork and bark, stone and pebbles, moss, dried leaves and seeds, beeswax, handmade paper.
- **Intentional.** Every production gets a written material bible: one connecting idea, a closed list of allowed materials, a palette, where each material may appear, and a list of what is never allowed. The Production Designer checks every new material against it. The Creative Director holds the house-level rules across productions.

## Candidate threads (Chris to choose or combine)

1. **Made from one place.** Everything in a film's world is built from what you'd find in one location (a potting shed, a riverbank, a grandmother's sewing box). The materials tell you where you are.
2. **The maker's hand shows.** Every material shows how it was made: fingerprints in clay, visible stitches in felt, knife marks in wood, brush strokes on painted skies. The thread is the process.
3. **One fiber runs through it.** A literal recurring thread (for example undyed wool yarn) appears in every scene: in costumes, holding sets together, stitched into skies. It can also carry story.
4. **Earth pigments only.** Every color comes from natural earth pigments (ochres, umbers, siennas, clay greens, charcoal, chalk, with one indigo or madder accent), so clay, cloth and wood share one palette.

Working proposal until Chris decides: 2 and 4 become house rules for every production; 1 or 3 is chosen per production as its creative concept.
